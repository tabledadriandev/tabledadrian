# Sambal Udang Petai, Prawns in Fiery Tamarind Sambal

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> A Nyonya-style prawn sambal, hot-sour-sweet, with optional petai beans for the brave — one of those small dishes that colonises the whole table's attention.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- raw king prawns, peeled, tails on — 600 g
- dried red chillies, soaked — 12
- fresh red chillies — 30 g
- shallots — 100 g
- garlic — 20 g
- shrimp paste (belacan), toasted — 10 g
- candlenuts or macadamias — 20 g
- tamarind paste — 30 g
- palm sugar — 25 g
- petai (stink beans), halved — 80 g (optional)
- onion, thick rings — 100 g
- vegetable oil — 60 ml
- fine sea salt — 6 g
- lime — 1

## Method
1. Blend both chillies, shallots, garlic, belacan and candlenuts with 40 ml water to a coarse paste.
2. Heat oil in a wok over medium heat; fry the paste 8–10 min, stirring, until it deepens to brick red and the oil pools at the edge (pecah minyak).
3. Add tamarind, palm sugar, salt and 100 ml water; simmer 4 min to a thick, glossy sambal.
4. Add petai if using; 2 min.
5. Raise heat; add prawns and onion rings.
6. Toss 3–4 min until the prawns curl and are thickly coated.
7. Finish with a squeeze of lime; taste — heat, sour and sweet should arrive in that order.

## Plating & Service
Pile into a shallow bowl with jasmine or coconut rice; the sambal should cling, with a slick of red oil as proof of proper frying.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Squid or hard-boiled eggs are classic alternates; drop belacan and use miso for shellfish-allergic tables (with tofu). The base sambal keeps a week chilled.
