# Tom Yum Goong Nam Khon, Creamy Hot & Sour Prawn Soup

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The rich 'nam khon' version of Thailand's iconic soup — prawn-shell stock, chilli jam and a touch of evaporated milk for the classic creamy blush.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- whole raw prawns — 700 g (heads and shells reserved)
- water — 1.2 L
- lemongrass, bruised, 5 cm batons — 40 g
- galangal, sliced — 25 g
- makrut lime leaves, torn — 6
- bird's-eye chillies, bruised — 4–6
- oyster or straw mushrooms, halved — 200 g
- cherry tomatoes, halved — 120 g
- nam prik pao (chilli jam) — 40 g
- fish sauce — 40 ml
- lime juice — 50 ml
- evaporated milk — 60 ml
- coriander leaves — 10 g
- vegetable oil — 15 ml

## Method
1. Peel and devein prawns. Fry heads and shells in the oil over high heat 3–4 min, crushing, until deep orange.
2. Add water; simmer 15 min, then strain — this stock is the soul of the soup.
3. Return stock to the pot with lemongrass, galangal, lime leaves and chillies; simmer 5 min to infuse.
4. Add mushrooms; 3 min. Add tomatoes and nam prik pao; 2 min.
5. Add prawns; poach 2 min until just pink.
6. Off the heat, season with fish sauce, then lime juice, then evaporated milk — never boil after the lime or it dulls.
7. Taste: hot first, sour hard on its heels, salty, faintly sweet-creamy.

## Plating & Service
Serve in deep bowls, aromatics left in for looks (warn guests), coriander on top; jasmine rice optional but wise.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Skip evaporated milk for the clear 'nam sai' version (dairy-free); chicken and shiitake make tom yum gai. Adjust chilli count per charter, not per recipe.
