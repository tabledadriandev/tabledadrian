# Opor Ayam, White Coconut Chicken with Lemongrass & Coriander Seed

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The gentle one — a pale, aromatic coconut braise with no chilli in the base, all fragrance from lemongrass, galangal and toasted coriander. Ideal for mixed tables.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- chicken thighs and drumsticks, bone-in — 1 kg
- shallots — 80 g
- garlic — 25 g
- candlenuts or macadamias — 30 g
- coriander seeds, toasted — 8 g
- cumin seeds, toasted — 2 g
- white pepper — 2 g
- galangal, bruised — 25 g
- ginger — 15 g
- lemongrass, bruised — 2 stalks
- makrut lime leaves — 4
- bay leaves (salam substitute) — 2
- coconut milk — 600 ml
- palm sugar — 12 g
- fine sea salt — 11 g
- vegetable oil — 40 ml
- crispy fried shallots — 25 g

## Method
1. Blend shallots, garlic, candlenuts, ground toasted coriander and cumin, white pepper and ginger to a paste.
2. Fry the paste in oil over medium heat 5–6 min — fragrant but still pale; opor stays ivory.
3. Add chicken; turn 4 min without browning.
4. Add coconut milk, galangal, lemongrass, lime leaves, bay, palm sugar, salt and 200 ml water.
5. Simmer gently, uncovered, 35–40 min, stirring now and then, until the chicken is tender and the sauce lightly thickened and cream-coloured.
6. Skim any split oil back in with a whisk.
7. Rest 10 min; the aroma settles into the meat.

## Plating & Service
Serve over rice or lontong-style rice cakes, a snow of crispy shallots on top; add a fiery sambal on the side for contrast.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Traditional at Lebaran with eggs added (simmer 15 min); duck legs need +30 min. Cashew works if candlenuts are unfindable.
