# Goong Ob Woonsen, Clay-Pot Glass Noodles with Prawns & Pancetta

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> A theatrical lidded-pot dish — glass noodles steam-braise over ginger and pork fat, drinking a peppery soy broth under a roof of prawns.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- raw king prawns, shell-on — 500 g
- glass (mung bean) noodles, soaked 15 min — 200 g dry
- pancetta or pork belly, thin slices — 80 g
- ginger, thick julienne — 30 g
- garlic, crushed — 20 g
- coriander roots, bruised — 10 g
- black peppercorns, coarse-cracked — 4 g
- light soy sauce — 30 ml
- oyster sauce — 25 g
- dark soy sauce — 8 ml
- sesame oil — 10 ml
- Chinese celery or celery leaves — 20 g
- chicken stock — 200 ml
- sugar — 8 g
- spring onion — 20 g

## Method
1. Whisk the braising sauce: both soys, oyster sauce, sesame oil, sugar and stock.
2. Line a heavy casserole or clay pot with the pancetta, then ginger, garlic, coriander roots and cracked pepper.
3. Drain the noodles and lay them over the aromatics; pour the sauce evenly through.
4. Arrange prawns on top in one layer; lid on.
5. Cook over medium heat 8 min — the pancetta renders, the noodles braise, the prawns steam.
6. Check: noodles glossy and translucent, prawns just curled; if dry, add 30 ml stock and steam 2 min more.
7. Rest, lidded, 3 min; fold once from the bottom so every strand is seasoned.

## Plating & Service
Bring the pot to the table and lift the lid there — the pepper-ginger steam is the first course. Celery leaves and spring onion over.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Blue crab or scallops instead of prawns; shiitake + smoked tofu replaces pork and shellfish. Glass noodles are naturally gluten-free but check the soy sauces.
