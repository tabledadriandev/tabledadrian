# Pad See Ew with Crispy Pork Belly & Chinese Broccoli

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Wide rice noodles charred against the wok until smoky-sweet, dragged through egg and dark soy, with crackling pork belly — the char (wok hei) is everything.

## Ingredients (serves 2)
- fresh wide rice noodles (sen yai), separated — 500 g
- cooked crispy pork belly, 2 cm chunks — 250 g
- Chinese broccoli (gai lan), stems sliced, leaves whole — 200 g
- eggs — 2
- garlic, chopped — 15 g
- dark soy sauce — 20 ml
- light soy sauce — 20 ml
- oyster sauce — 25 g
- white sugar — 10 g
- white vinegar — 10 ml
- vegetable oil — 45 ml
- white pepper — 1 g

## Method
1. Mix both soys, oyster sauce, sugar and vinegar into one sauce.
2. Heat the wok until smoking with 20 ml oil; sear the noodles in one layer, undisturbed, 60–90 seconds until charred in spots. Turn once, char again. Remove.
3. Add 15 ml oil; fry garlic 10 seconds, add gai lan stems 60 seconds, then leaves 30 seconds.
4. Push aside; crack in the eggs with remaining oil, scramble hard 30 seconds.
5. Return noodles with the sauce; toss over maximum heat 60–90 seconds until every ribbon is mahogany and edges catch.
6. Fold in the pork belly to heat through, 30 seconds.
7. Finish with white pepper.

## Plating & Service
Straight from wok to plate — this dish ages in seconds. Chilli vinegar (prik nam som) on the side is traditional.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Chicken or tofu instead of pork; dried noodles (soaked) work but char less. Cook one portion at a time — crowding kills the smoke.
