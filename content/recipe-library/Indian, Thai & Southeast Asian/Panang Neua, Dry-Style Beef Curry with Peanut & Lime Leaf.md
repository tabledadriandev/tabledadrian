# Panang Neua, Dry-Style Beef Curry with Peanut & Lime Leaf

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Thicker, sweeter and more perfumed than red curry — panang clings to seared beef like a satay-scented lacquer, finished with lime-leaf chiffonade.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- beef sirloin or bavette, sliced 5 mm — 600 g
- dried long red chillies, seeded, soaked — 10
- shallots — 40 g
- garlic — 20 g
- galangal — 12 g
- lemongrass — 20 g
- coriander root — 8 g
- makrut lime zest — 2 g
- shrimp paste — 6 g
- roasted peanuts, ground — 40 g
- coconut cream — 350 ml
- fish sauce — 25 ml
- palm sugar — 30 g
- makrut lime leaves, fine chiffonade — 6
- red chilli, slivered — 1
- Thai basil — 10 g

## Method
1. Pound the paste from chillies, lemongrass, galangal, coriander root, lime zest, garlic, shallots and shrimp paste; work in the ground peanuts at the end.
2. Crack 150 ml of the coconut cream in a wok over medium-high heat until the oil separates, 4–5 min.
3. Fry the paste in the cracked cream 4 min until it smells rounded and toasty.
4. Sear the beef in the paste over high heat 2 min — just coloured.
5. Add remaining coconut cream, palm sugar and fish sauce; simmer only 5–6 min. Panang should be thick enough to coat the meat, not soupy.
6. Taste: sweeter than a red curry, salt right behind, minimal sour.
7. Off the heat, fold in half the lime leaves and the basil.

## Plating & Service
Serve heaped, not flooded, with jasmine rice; finish with the rest of the lime-leaf chiffonade and chilli slivers — the aroma is the garnish.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Excellent with pork neck or portobello + tofu. For nut-free, replace peanuts with toasted sunflower seeds ground fine.
