# Semur Daging, Javanese Sweet-Soy Braised Beef with Nutmeg & Clove

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Java's Dutch-influenced dark stew — kecap manis, nutmeg and clove wrap beef in a glossy, gently sweet gravy that tastes like colonial history done right.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- beef chuck, 4 cm dice — 1.2 kg
- shallots — 100 g
- garlic — 25 g
- tomatoes, chopped — 200 g
- kecap manis — 90 ml
- light soy sauce — 15 ml
- nutmeg, freshly grated — 2 g
- cloves — 4
- white pepper — 2 g
- cinnamon stick — 1 (5 cm)
- ginger, bruised — 20 g
- water or stock — 800 ml
- waxy potatoes, halved — 400 g
- fried shallots — 25 g
- vegetable oil — 40 ml
- fine sea salt — 10 g

## Method
1. Blend shallots and garlic; fry in oil 4–5 min until soft and sweet.
2. Add beef; sear 6 min in the paste.
3. Add tomatoes, kecap manis, soy, nutmeg, cloves, cinnamon, ginger, white pepper, salt and the water.
4. Simmer, covered, 2 h until the beef relaxes.
5. Add potatoes; uncovered, 25–30 min, letting the gravy reduce to a dark, syrupy coat.
6. Fish out the whole spices; taste — sweetness should be rounded, not cloying, with warmth from nutmeg.
7. Rest 10 min.

## Plating & Service
Bowl with rice, fried shallots over; a bright tomato-chilli sambal alongside stops the sweetness settling.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Beef shin or oxtail (+45 min) go deeper; hard-boiled eggs are a classic addition. Gluten-free tamari + palm sugar can stand in for kecap manis.
