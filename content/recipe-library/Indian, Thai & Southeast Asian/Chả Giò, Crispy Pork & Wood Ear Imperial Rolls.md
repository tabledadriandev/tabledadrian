# Chả Giò, Crispy Pork & Wood Ear Imperial Rolls

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Vietnam's fried roll — a pork, prawn and wood-ear filling with glass noodles, fried until deeply golden, then wrapped cold in lettuce and mint and dunked in nước chấm.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- spring roll pastry or rice paper, 20 cm — 20
- pork mince — 350 g
- raw prawns, chopped — 150 g
- glass noodles, soaked, snipped — 50 g dry
- wood ear mushrooms, soaked, fine-sliced — 15 g dry
- carrot, fine grate — 80 g
- onion, fine dice — 60 g
- egg — 1
- fish sauce — 20 ml
- white pepper — 2 g
- sugar — 5 g
- oil for frying — 1 L
- lettuce + mint + coriander, to wrap — 250 g
- nước chấm — 200 ml

## Method
1. Squeeze the grated carrot and diced onion in a cloth — dry filling means crisp rolls.
2. Mix pork, prawns, noodles, wood ear, carrot, onion, egg, fish sauce, pepper and sugar; rest 15 min.
3. Roll 35 g filling per wrapper into tight cigars, sealing with water (or beaten egg for pastry).
4. Fry at 160 °C, 6–7 min, until cooked through and pale gold; rest on a rack.
5. Second fry at 185 °C, 60–90 seconds, to a deep, blistered bronze.
6. Drain upright in a rack so steam escapes from the ends.
7. Serve hot with the wrap-and-dip station.

## Plating & Service
Guests wrap each hot roll in cold lettuce with herbs and drag through nước chấm — the hot-cold crunch is the whole point.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Crab replaces prawn for luxury; a mushroom-tofu filling converts it. Rice-paper versions fry lacier but demand a gentler first fry (150 °C).
