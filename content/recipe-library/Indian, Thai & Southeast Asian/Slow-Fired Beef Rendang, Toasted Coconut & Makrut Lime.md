# Slow-Fired Beef Rendang, Toasted Coconut & Makrut Lime

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> True Minang rendang — the beef braises until the coconut milk fries it in its own oil, dark, dry-ish and profound. Four hours of mostly ignoring a pot.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- beef chuck or shin, 5 cm chunks — 1.5 kg
- coconut milk — 800 ml
- dried red chillies, soaked — 15
- shallots — 120 g
- garlic — 40 g
- ginger — 30 g
- galangal — 30 g
- fresh turmeric (or 5 g ground) — 20 g
- lemongrass, bruised — 3 stalks
- makrut lime leaves — 6
- turmeric leaf if found (optional) — 1
- desiccated coconut — 40 g
- tamarind paste — 20 g
- palm sugar — 20 g
- fine sea salt — 14 g

## Method
1. Toast the desiccated coconut in a dry pan to deep gold, 5–6 min; grind to an oily paste (kerisik).
2. Blend chillies, shallots, garlic, ginger, galangal and turmeric with 50 ml water to a smooth paste.
3. In a wide heavy pan, combine beef, spice paste, coconut milk, lemongrass, lime leaves and salt. Bring to a simmer — no browning first; rendang builds differently.
4. Simmer uncovered over low heat, stirring every 20 min, about 2 h, as the liquid reduces and turns from soup to gravy.
5. Add kerisik, tamarind and palm sugar; keep reducing, stirring more often, 1–1.5 h more.
6. Critical stage: the oil splits out and the beef begins to fry in it. Stir constantly now, 15–20 min, as everything darkens to deep mahogany.
7. Rest 20 min minimum; better tomorrow, best the day after.

## Plating & Service
Serve modest portions — it's intense — with coconut rice, cucumber and something green; garnish with a fried lime leaf.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Short rib and lamb shoulder are superb; jackfruit rendang for plant-based (90 min total). Never rush the final fry stage — that's where rendang becomes rendang.
