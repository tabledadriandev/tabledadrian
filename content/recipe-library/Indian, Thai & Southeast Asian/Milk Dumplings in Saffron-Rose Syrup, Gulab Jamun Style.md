# Milk Dumplings in Saffron-Rose Syrup, Gulab Jamun Style

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Feather-light milk-solid dumplings fried low and slow, then soaked in a saffron-rose syrup — the temperature discipline of the fry is the entire craft.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- milk powder (full-fat) — 160 g
- plain flour — 45 g
- fine semolina — 10 g
- baking powder — 2 g
- ghee, soft — 20 g
- whole milk — about 70 ml
- caster sugar — 350 g
- water — 350 ml
- saffron — 0.2 g
- green cardamom, bruised — 5 pods
- lemon juice — 10 ml
- rose water — 5 ml
- pistachios, slivered — 15 g
- ghee or oil for frying — 1 L

## Method
1. Syrup first: dissolve sugar in water with cardamom and saffron; simmer 5 min — sticky but pourable, NOT thread stage. Add lemon (stops crystallising) and rose water; keep warm.
2. Rub milk powder, flour, semolina, baking powder and soft ghee together.
3. Add milk a spoon at a time to a soft, slightly tacky dough. Do not knead — fold until just smooth. Rest 10 min.
4. Roll into 18 crack-free balls with greased palms — any crack becomes a rupture in the fryer.
5. Heat ghee to 130–140 °C — this low temperature is everything.
6. Fry in batches, stirring the oil (not the balls) so they rotate and colour evenly, 7–9 min to deep amber.
7. Slip straight into the warm (not hot) syrup; soak 2 h minimum — they double in size.

## Plating & Service
Serve two per bowl, warm, with syrup, pistachios over — vanilla ice cream alongside is inauthentic and universally welcomed.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> They keep 4 days in syrup, improving. If a test ball splits, add 5 g flour; if dense, a drop more milk. Temperature over everything.
