# Sambal Matah, Raw Balinese Shallot & Lemongrass Relish

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Bali's raw sambal — fine-sliced shallots, lemongrass, chilli and lime leaf bruised with hot coconut oil; explosive freshness over grilled fish or chicken.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- shallots, sliced paper-thin — 100 g
- lemongrass, tender core only, sliced hair-thin — 25 g
- red bird's-eye chillies, fine rounds — 10 g
- makrut lime leaves, hair-fine chiffonade — 4
- garlic, fine-sliced — 8 g
- shrimp paste, roasted (optional) — 4 g
- fine sea salt — 4 g
- caster sugar — 3 g
- lime juice — 25 ml
- coconut oil — 45 ml

## Method
1. Slice everything genuinely fine — matah is knife-work; ragged cuts read raw and harsh.
2. Soak the shallots in iced water 5 min; drain and dry (keeps them crisp, softens the bite).
3. Combine shallots, lemongrass, chillies, lime leaf, garlic, crumbled shrimp paste, salt and sugar.
4. Squeeze and bruise the mix lightly with clean hands, 30 seconds — bruised, not crushed.
5. Heat the coconut oil until shimmering (about 170 °C).
6. Pour the hot oil over; it will hiss and half-cook everything. Toss immediately.
7. Lime juice in; taste — bright, salty, aromatic. Serve within the hour.

## Plating & Service
Spoon generously over grilled fish, ayam bakar, fried eggs or even burrata if you're feeling cross-cultural.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Skip shrimp paste for vegan (add a pinch more salt). It does not keep — make it à la minute, which takes 15 minutes anyway.
