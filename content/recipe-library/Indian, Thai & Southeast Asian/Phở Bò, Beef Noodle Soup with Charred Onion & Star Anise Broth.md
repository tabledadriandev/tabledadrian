# Phở Bò, Beef Noodle Soup with Charred Onion & Star Anise Broth

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The five-hour broth is non-negotiable — charred aromatics, toasted spices, and marrow bones give the clear, sweet-savoury depth that defines a proper phở.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- beef marrow and knuckle bones — 2 kg
- beef brisket — 600 g
- sirloin, semi-frozen, shaved thin — 300 g
- onions, halved — 300 g
- ginger, split lengthways — 80 g
- star anise — 5
- cinnamon stick — 1 (8 cm)
- black cardamom — 2
- coriander seeds — 8 g
- cloves — 4
- fish sauce — 70 ml
- rock or palm sugar — 25 g
- fine sea salt — 15 g
- flat rice noodles (bánh phở), soaked/cooked — 600 g
- spring onion, sliced — 40 g
- white onion, shaved paper-thin — 60 g
- garnish plate: Thai basil, mint, bean sprouts, lime, sliced chilli — 300 g

## Method
1. Blanch bones 5 min in boiling water; drain, rinse — this guarantees a clear broth.
2. Char onion halves and ginger under a 250 °C grill (or open flame) 8–10 min until blackened; peel the worst of the char.
3. Toast star anise, cinnamon, cardamom, coriander seeds and cloves 2 min; tie in muslin.
4. Cover bones and brisket with 4 L cold water; bring to a bare shiver (90–95 °C, never boiling), skimming for the first 20 min.
5. Add charred aromatics, spice bag and salt; hold at a shiver 4–5 h, skimming occasionally. Pull brisket at 2 h; chill it.
6. Strain the broth; season with fish sauce and sugar until it tastes deep, clean and quietly sweet.
7. Build bowls: hot noodles, sliced brisket, raw shaved sirloin; ladle 400 ml boiling broth over — it cooks the raw beef rosé. Top with both onions.

## Plating & Service
Serve immediately with the herb-and-lime plate so guests season their own bowl — the ritual is half the dish.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Chicken phở (gà): whole bird, 90-min broth, same aromatics. Broth freezes beautifully — make double for the crossing.
