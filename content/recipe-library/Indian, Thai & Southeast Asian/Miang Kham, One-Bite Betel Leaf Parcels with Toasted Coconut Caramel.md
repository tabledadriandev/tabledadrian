# Miang Kham, One-Bite Betel Leaf Parcels with Toasted Coconut Caramel

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The perfect canapé already exists and it's Thai — leaves piled with lime, ginger, chilli, peanut and toasted coconut, bound by a sticky galangal caramel.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- betel leaves (or baby spinach / small chard leaves) — 24
- fresh coconut, shaved, or desiccated — 60 g
- roasted peanuts — 60 g
- dried shrimp (optional; omit for vegetarian) — 20 g
- lime, skin on, tiny dice — 80 g
- ginger, tiny dice — 40 g
- shallots, tiny dice — 50 g
- bird's-eye chillies, thin rounds — 8 g
- palm sugar — 100 g
- fish sauce (or soy) — 20 ml
- galangal, grated — 10 g
- water — 60 ml
- tamarind paste — 10 g

## Method
1. Toast the coconut in a dry pan to deep gold, 4–5 min; reserve.
2. Make the sauce: melt palm sugar with water, galangal, tamarind and fish sauce; simmer 6–8 min to a loose caramel that ribbons off a spoon. Stir in a third of the coconut; cool to warm.
3. Dice everything small and uniform — this is knife-work mise en place at its most honest.
4. Arrange leaves shiny-side down on a platter.
5. Drop into each: a pinch of coconut, peanuts, lime (skin on — it's the point), ginger, shallot, chilli, dried shrimp if using.
6. Spoon a scant teaspoon of caramel on each just before serving.
7. Fold-and-pop is the guests' job.

## Plating & Service
Serve as a DIY platter — components in bowls, sauce in the centre — or pre-built for a canapé round; one bite, whole flavour spectrum.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Spinach leaves work everywhere betel doesn't grow; drop the shrimp and use soy for a fully plant-based version.
