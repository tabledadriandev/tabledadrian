# Slow-Roast Pork Belly with Crackling & Bramley Apple Sauce

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Three hours low, one blast high: glass crackling over spoon-soft meat, cut by the sharpest apple sauce in the repertoire.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- Pork belly, skin on, scored at 1 cm — 1.8 kg
- Coarse sea salt — 20 g
- Fennel seeds — 8 g, crushed
- Black peppercorns — 4 g, crushed
- Garlic — 4 cloves (20 g), smashed
- Onions — 300 g, thick rings
- Dry cider — 300 ml
- Chicken stock — 400 ml
- Bramley apples — 500 g, peeled, chopped
- Caster sugar — 30 g
- Lemon juice — 15 ml
- Bay leaves — 2
- Fine sea salt — 4 g

## Method
1. Dry the skin thoroughly; rub 15 g coarse salt into the score lines. Rest uncovered in the fridge 4–24 hours to dehydrate the skin.
2. Heat oven to 150°C. Brush salt off; rub the flesh (not skin) with fennel, pepper and 5 g salt.
3. Bed the belly on onion rings, garlic and bay in a snug tray; pour cider and stock around (never on the skin).
4. Roast 3 hours until a skewer meets no resistance. Top up liquid if the tray dries.
5. Meanwhile, simmer apples with sugar, lemon juice and 20 ml water, lidded, 10–12 minutes to a sharp, barely-sweet purée.
6. Raise oven to 240°C (or grill); blast the belly 15–20 minutes until the skin puffs to glass. Watch constantly.
7. Rest 15 minutes. Strain and skim tray juices for a light gravy; season.
8. Portion with a serrated knife through the crackling, then a straight blade through the meat.

## Plating & Service
A square of belly per plate, crackling intact and upright, apple sauce and jus alongside — greens and mash complete it.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Asian detour: rub with five-spice and serve with pak choi. The apple sauce must stay tart — it is the knife that cuts the fat.
