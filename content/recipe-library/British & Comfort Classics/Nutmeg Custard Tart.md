# Nutmeg Custard Tart

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The old English custard tart at its most refined — a deep, trembling set at exactly the right temperature under a galaxy of fresh nutmeg.

## Ingredients (serves 8)
- Plain flour — 220 g
- Unsalted butter — 130 g, cold, diced
- Icing sugar — 45 g
- Egg yolk — 1, plus 9 yolks for the custard
- Cold water — 25 ml
- Whole milk — 250 ml
- Double cream — 450 ml
- Caster sugar — 90 g
- Vanilla pod — half, split
- Whole nutmeg — 1, for heavy grating
- Fine sea salt — 2 g

## Method
1. Pastry: rub flour, butter, icing sugar and salt to crumbs; bind with 1 yolk and water. Chill 30 minutes.
2. Line a 20 cm deep tart ring at 3 mm with overhang; chill 20 minutes. Blind-bake at 180°C with beans 20 minutes; remove beans, 8 minutes more. Seal any cracks with reserved raw pastry; brush with yolk wash and bake 2 minutes.
3. Custard: warm milk, cream and vanilla to 60°C. Whisk 9 yolks with caster sugar without aerating; pour on the warm cream, stirring, not whisking.
4. Strain twice; skim every bubble from the surface.
5. Set the tart case in the oven at 130°C; pour the custard in situ to the very brim. Grate nutmeg generously across the entire surface.
6. Bake 35–45 minutes until set at the edges with a definite central wobble (core 78–80°C).
7. Cool 2 hours at room temperature — never refrigerate before first service. Trim the overhang with a serrated knife.

## Plating & Service
Bare, proud slices — the cut face should sheen and tremble. Nothing alongside; this tart tolerates no garnish.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> The nutmeg must be fresh-grated — pre-ground is a different (lesser) spice. For burnt-honey custard, replace 30 g sugar with dark honey warmed to amber.
