# Cider-Brined Pork Chop with Burnt Apple Puree

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> A four-hour cider brine keeps thick chops juicy at 62°C, while blackened apples blitz into a smoky-sweet purée that replaces any sauce.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- Pork chops, bone in, 4 cm thick — 4 x 300 g
- Dry cider — 500 ml
- Water — 500 ml
- Fine sea salt — 45 g (brine) plus 3 g
- Light brown sugar — 30 g
- Bay leaves — 2; black peppercorns — 6
- Eating apples — 4 (600 g), halved
- Olive oil — 40 ml
- Cider vinegar — 15 ml
- Unsalted sage leaves — 8
- Chicken stock — 150 ml
- Hispi cabbage — 1 (600 g), quartered
- Black pepper — 2 g

## Method
1. Brine: dissolve 45 g salt and sugar in cider and water with bay and peppercorns. Submerge chops 4 hours (no longer). Rinse; pat dry; rest uncovered 30 minutes.
2. Burnt apple: char apple halves cut-side down in a dry ovenproof pan over high heat 6–8 minutes until blackened; transfer to a 180°C oven 10 minutes until collapsing. Blend flesh (skins too) with vinegar, 10 ml oil and a pinch of salt.
3. Sear chops in 20 ml oil over high heat 3 minutes per side, standing them on the fat edge 2 minutes to render.
4. Add sage; roast at 180°C to a core of 60–62°C, 6–8 minutes. Rest 8 minutes (carryover to 65°C).
5. Char hispi quarters cut-side down in the chop pan 4 minutes; add stock, cover, steam 4 minutes.
6. Reduce resting juices with remaining stock 2 minutes for a light jus.

## Plating & Service
A swipe of burnt apple, the chop over, hispi wedge alongside, jus and crisp sage on top.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> No time to brine? Dry-salt 1 hour. The purée is equally brilliant with roast pork belly or duck.
