# Ham Hock Terrine with Piccalilli

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> A pressed terrine bound only in its own gelatinous stock, with sharp homemade piccalilli — the great British charcuterie plate.

## Ingredients (serves 8)
- Smoked ham hocks — 2 (about 2 kg)
- Onion — 150 g, halved
- Carrot — 100 g
- Celery — 60 g
- Bay leaves — 2
- Black peppercorns — 8
- Flat-leaf parsley — 25 g, chopped
- Dijon mustard — 15 g
- Cider vinegar — 20 ml
- Cauliflower — 200 g, tiny florets
- Silverskin onions — 100 g, halved
- Cucumber — 100 g, diced
- English mustard powder — 8 g; ground turmeric — 4 g; cornflour — 12 g
- White wine vinegar — 200 ml; caster sugar — 80 g

## Method
1. Cover hocks with cold water; add onion, carrot, celery, bay and peppercorns. Simmer gently 3 hours until the meat waves off the bone.
2. Strain; reduce 500 ml of the liquor by half to concentrate the gelatine.
3. Piccalilli: salt cauliflower, onions and cucumber 1 hour; rinse. Boil vinegar and sugar; whisk in mustard powder, turmeric and slaked cornflour; simmer 2 minutes, fold in vegetables, cook 1 minute — they must stay crunchy. Jar and cool.
4. Pick the ham into generous shreds, discarding skin and fat; season with mustard, cider vinegar, parsley and pepper.
5. Pack into a lined 1 L terrine mould; pour over warm reduced liquor to just cover.
6. Press with a weighted board; chill 12 hours until sliceable.

## Plating & Service
Cut thick slices with a hot knife; serve with a spoon of piccalilli, dressed leaves and grilled sourdough.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Fold in soft-herb gherkins or capers. The terrine keeps 5 days; piccalilli improves for weeks.
