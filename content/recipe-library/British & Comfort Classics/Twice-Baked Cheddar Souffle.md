# Twice-Baked Cheddar Souffle

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The chef's cheat that looks like a high-wire act — bake ahead, then flood with cream and re-bake to order so it rises twice, stress-free.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- Whole milk — 350 ml
- Bay leaf — 1
- Unsalted butter — 50 g, plus 20 g for moulds
- Plain flour — 50 g
- Mature Cheddar — 180 g, grated, plus 30 g for gratinating
- English mustard — 8 g
- Egg yolks — 4
- Egg whites — 5 (about 165 g)
- Double cream — 300 ml
- Parmesan — 30 g, finely grated, for moulds
- Freshly grated nutmeg — 0.5 g
- Fine sea salt — 5 g
- Cayenne pepper — 1 g

## Method
1. Butter six 150 ml ramekins and coat with Parmesan. Heat oven to 180°C; set a bain-marie tray inside.
2. Infuse milk with bay 10 minutes. Make a roux with 50 g butter and flour, 2 minutes; whisk in the strained milk. Cook 3 minutes to a thick panade.
3. Off heat, beat in 180 g Cheddar, mustard, nutmeg, salt, cayenne, then yolks one at a time.
4. Whisk whites to soft peaks with a pinch of salt; fold in one-third to loosen, then the rest gently.
5. Fill ramekins to 1 cm below the rim; bake in the bain-marie 20 minutes until risen and set. Cool — they will sink; this is correct. (Hold up to 24 h chilled.)
6. To serve: heat oven to 200°C. Unmould soufflés into a gratin dish or individual dishes.
7. Pour 50 ml cream over each, scatter the extra Cheddar, bake 10–12 minutes until re-risen, glazed and bubbling.

## Plating & Service
Serve in the baking dish, cream still seething at the edges, with a sharp apple and watercress salad to cut the richness.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Swap half the Cheddar for Stilton and add chopped walnuts on top. GF: rice-flour roux works perfectly here.
