# Braised Beef Cheeks in Stout with Horseradish Mash

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Cheeks turn to sticky ribbons after four hours in stout; horseradish in the mash cuts the richness like a blade.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- Beef cheeks, trimmed — 4 (about 1.2 kg)
- Plain flour — 30 g
- Olive oil — 40 ml
- Onions — 250 g, chunky
- Carrots — 200 g, chunky
- Celery — 60 g, sliced
- Garlic — 4 cloves (20 g)
- Tomato purée — 30 g
- Stout — 500 ml
- Beef stock — 500 ml
- Star anise — 1; bay — 2; thyme — 4 sprigs
- Floury potatoes — 1 kg, peeled
- Unsalted butter — 90 g; whole milk — 100 ml, hot
- Fresh horseradish — 25 g, grated (or 40 g horseradish cream)
- Fine sea salt — 9 g

## Method
1. Season and flour the cheeks; brown hard in oil on all faces, 8 minutes total. Remove.
2. Colour onions, carrots and celery 8 minutes; add garlic and tomato purée, 2 minutes.
3. Return cheeks; add stout, stock, star anise, bay, thyme. Braise covered at 140°C for 3.5–4 hours until a spoon cuts them.
4. Lift cheeks; strain and reduce the liquor by half to a glossy sauce. Season; return cheeks to glaze over low heat, basting.
5. Mash: boil potatoes 20 minutes, rice, beat in butter and hot milk; fold in horseradish and salt at the end.
6. Rest the glazed cheeks 5 minutes in the sauce off the heat.

## Plating & Service
One cheek per plate over horseradish mash, sauce spooned until it pools, charred hispi or buttered kale alongside.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> GF: dust with cornflour instead. The braise freezes beautifully — always make double for the crew.
