# Ras el Hanout, Top-of-the-Shop Blend of Twenty Spices

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> "Head of the shop" — every Marrakech spice merchant guards his own; this version balances warm, floral and pungent in thirds, with rose petals doing the perfume work.

## Ingredients (makes ~110 g)
- Coriander seeds — 14 g
- Cumin seeds — 12 g
- Black peppercorns — 8 g
- Allspice berries — 6 g
- Green cardamom seeds — 5 g
- Cassia/cinnamon — 10 g
- Cloves — 2 g
- Fennel seeds — 5 g
- Aniseed — 3 g
- Ground ginger — 12 g
- Turmeric — 8 g
- Sweet paprika — 8 g
- Ground nutmeg — 4 g
- Ground mace — 2 g
- Cayenne — 2 g
- Dried rose petals — 6 g
- Dried lavender (optional, tiny) — 1 g
- Ground galangal (optional) — 2 g

## Method
1. Toast all whole spices (coriander through aniseed) over medium heat 2–3 min until aromatic; cool fully.
2. Grind the toasted spices with the rose petals (and lavender if using) to a fine powder.
3. Sieve, regrind the coarse remainder.
4. Whisk in every pre-ground spice until evenly ochre.
5. Rest the blend 24 h in a sealed jar before first use — it needs to marry. Keeps 8 weeks dark.

## Plating & Service
The signature of mrouzia, tagines and grilled quail in this library; also astonishing rubbed on roast carrots or stirred into honey for glazing.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Heat-shy tables: halve the cayenne and pepper. A 1:4 mix with fine salt makes an instant rotisserie rub.
