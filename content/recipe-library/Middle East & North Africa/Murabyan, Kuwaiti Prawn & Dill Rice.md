# Murabyan, Kuwaiti Prawn & Dill Rice

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Kuwait's fisherman's pilaf — prawns twice-used: shells for the broth, tails folded through dill-turmeric rice with a crown of caramelised onions; loomi keeps it unmistakably Gulf.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- Large prawns, shell-on — 900 g
- Basmati rice, soaked — 450 g
- Onions, sliced — 350 g
- Neutral oil — 80 ml
- Garlic, grated — 15 g
- Ginger, grated — 12 g
- Turmeric — 5 g
- Gulf baharat — 8 g
- Ground loomi — 3 g
- Dill, chopped — 40 g
- Coriander, chopped — 15 g
- Green chilli — 10 g
- Fine sea salt — 16 g
- Water — 900 ml
- Lime — 1

## Method
1. Peel prawns; simmer shells and heads in the water 15 min; strain — this is the rice liquor. Season prawns with 4 g salt, 2 g turmeric.
2. Fry onions in oil to deep bronze, 18 min; reserve half for the crown.
3. To the pot onions add garlic, ginger, chilli, baharat, loomi and remaining turmeric; 1 min.
4. Sear prawns in the spiced onions 90 seconds — barely coloured, not cooked through; remove.
5. Add rice, dill, coriander, remaining salt and 800 ml shell broth. Boil 3 min, cover on lowest heat 10 min.
6. Bury prawns in the rice, re-cover, rest off heat 10 min — carryover finishes them at ~55 °C, juicy.

## Plating & Service
Fork gently onto a platter so prawns surface, crown with reserved onions, lime wedges around. A cold yogurt-cucumber side plays against the spice.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> No shells? Use light fish stock. Substitute half the dill with coriander for a greener, brighter version.
