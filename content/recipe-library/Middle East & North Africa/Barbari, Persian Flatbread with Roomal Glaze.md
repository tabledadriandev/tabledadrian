# Barbari, Persian Flatbread with Roomal Glaze

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The long, ridged Tehran breakfast bread — its bronze sheen comes from roomal, a cooked flour-and-bicarb slurry painted on before baking; the finger-drawn channels are structural, not decorative.

## Ingredients (serves 6, makes 2 loaves)
- Strong flour — 500 g
- Instant yeast — 5 g
- Sugar — 6 g
- Fine sea salt — 10 g
- Warm water (35 °C) — 340 ml
- Olive oil — 20 ml
- Roomal: flour 10 g, bicarbonate of soda 1 g, water 100 ml
- Sesame seeds — 15 g
- Nigella seeds — 5 g

## Method
1. Knead flour, yeast, sugar, salt, water and oil 10 min to a soft, extensible dough; bulk prove 90 min with one fold at 45 min.
2. Roomal: whisk the flour, bicarb and water; simmer 2 min to a thin glue; cool.
3. Divide dough in two; shape into logs and rest 20 min. Heat a steel at 260 °C.
4. Stretch each log to a 35 × 12 cm oval on parchment. Paint generously with roomal.
5. Drag oiled fingertips firmly down the length to make 4–5 deep channels; scatter sesame and nigella.
6. Bake 12–15 min until the ridges are deep bronze and the channels pale gold.

## Plating & Service
Serve warm, torn across the ridges, with feta, walnuts, honey and sweet tea — the classic Persian sobhaneh.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Halve for four short loaves for a bread basket. Day-old barbari, toasted, is the right base for borani spreads.
