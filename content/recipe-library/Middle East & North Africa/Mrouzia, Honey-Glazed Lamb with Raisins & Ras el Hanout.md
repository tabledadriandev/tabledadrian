# Mrouzia, Honey-Glazed Lamb with Raisins & Ras el Hanout

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The Eid confit of Morocco — lamb shanks cooked until lacquered in honey, raisins and a heavy hand of ras el hanout; unapologetically sweet, balanced by salt and long cooking, portioned small like a rich terrine course.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- Lamb shanks or shoulder on the bone — 1800 g
- Ras el hanout — 18 g
- Ground ginger — 5 g
- Saffron, steeped in 30 ml hot water — 0.3 g
- Fine sea salt — 15 g
- Olive oil — 50 ml
- Onion, grated — 200 g
- Water — 700 ml
- Raisins or sultanas — 200 g
- Honey — 90 g
- Ground cinnamon — 3 g
- Blanched almonds, fried — 80 g
- Sesame seeds, toasted — 10 g

## Method
1. Rub lamb with ras el hanout, ginger, salt and oil; rest 2 h or overnight.
2. Set in a heavy pot with onion, saffron water and water; braise covered at 150 °C (or bare simmer) for 2.5 h until sliding off the bone.
3. Add raisins, honey and cinnamon; cook uncovered, basting, 25–35 min, until the liquid reduces to a dark, syrupy glaze that coats the meat like lacquer.
4. Watch the final 10 min closely — honey burns fast; the glaze should be mahogany, not black.
5. Fry almonds at 160 °C to gold.

## Plating & Service
Small portions, glazed and glistening, with almonds and sesame over; plain couscous or bread cuts the richness. A salad of bitter leaves alongside is the right instinct.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Nut-free: finish with sesame only. Serve leftover mrouzia shredded in warm msemen as the world's best flatbread wrap.
