# Chelow with Saffron Tahdig, The Persian Crust

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The gold-standard technique card — parboil, steam under a towel-wrapped lid, and sacrifice a layer to the pan gods; the tahdig should release in one amber disc that cracks like crème brûlée.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- Aged basmati rice — 500 g
- Fine sea salt — 40 g (mostly for the parboil water)
- Water (parboil) — 2500 ml
- Neutral oil — 60 ml
- Unsalted butter — 30 g
- Saffron, ground, steeped in 50 ml hot water — 0.5 g
- Greek yogurt — 40 g
- Water (for steaming) — 30 ml

## Method
1. Wash rice in five changes of water until it runs clear; soak 1–4 h in salted water.
2. Boil the 2.5 L water with 35 g salt (sea-water salty). Add drained rice; boil hard 4–6 min until the outside is soft but the core has bite. Test constantly.
3. Drain and rinse briefly with warm water.
4. Whisk 150 g of the parboiled rice with yogurt, half the saffron water and 30 ml oil — this is the tahdig layer.
5. Heat remaining oil in the heavy pot; spread the tahdig mix evenly, then mound the rest of the rice into a pyramid. Poke 5 steam holes; dot with butter and sprinkle 30 ml water.
6. High heat 8–10 min until the base sizzles and steam rises from the holes; then wrap the lid in a towel and cook on lowest heat 40–45 min.
7. Spoon remaining saffron water over the crown. Rest off heat 5 min; dip the pot base in cold water 30 seconds to release the crust.

## Plating & Service
Fluff the white rice onto a platter, top with the saffron-stained crown, and present the tahdig disc whole for cracking at the table.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Vegan tahdig: replace yogurt with 30 g extra oil, or line with lavash or 3 mm potato slices. The potato version is the most forgiving for rough seas.
