# Mansaf-Style Lamb in Fermented Yogurt Sauce with Almond Rice

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Jordan's national dish adapted for Riviera larders — lamb braised, then finished in a stabilised sheep-yogurt sauce standing in for jameed; the sauce must be whisked one direction over heat or it splits, as every Jordanian grandmother will tell you.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- Lamb shoulder, large bone-in pieces — 1800 g
- Sheep or goat yogurt — 800 g
- Cornflour — 12 g
- Egg white — 30 g (1)
- Onion, halved — 150 g
- Cardamom pods — 6
- Bay leaves — 2
- Fine sea salt — 20 g
- Turmeric — 2 g
- Basmati rice — 450 g
- Neutral oil or ghee — 60 ml
- Flaked almonds, fried — 60 g
- Pine nuts, fried — 30 g
- Flatbread (shrak or lavash) — 200 g
- Flat-leaf parsley — 10 g

## Method
1. Simmer lamb with onion, cardamom, bay and 12 g salt in 1.5 L water, skimming, 2 h until tender. Strain; keep 500 ml broth.
2. Whisk yogurt smooth with cornflour and egg white (the stabilisers). Bring to a simmer in a wide pot, whisking constantly in one direction, 5 min.
3. Thin with the reserved broth to a light cream; season. Return lamb; simmer gently 20 min so the meat drinks the tang.
4. Rice: cook in salted water with turmeric and oil (absorption method: boil 3 min, covered lowest heat 11 min, rest 10 min).
5. Fry the nuts golden in oil at 160 °C.

## Plating & Service
Line the platter with flatbread moistened with yogurt sauce, then rice, then lamb; ladle sauce over generously and finish with nuts and parsley. Extra sauce in a jug — guests will ask.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> True jameed (if sourced) replaces the yogurt: soak 24 h and blend. Serve smaller as a plated main with the sauce foamed for fine-dining service.
