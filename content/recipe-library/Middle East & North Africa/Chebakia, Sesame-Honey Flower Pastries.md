# Chebakia, Sesame-Honey Flower Pastries

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Ramadan's rose-shaped pastry — anise-saffron dough folded into flowers, fried, then drowned in hot orange-blossom honey until lacquered; the fold looks impossible once, then becomes a rhythm.

## Ingredients (serves 12, makes ~30)
- Plain flour — 400 g
- Sesame seeds, toasted and ground — 60 g (plus 30 g whole, to finish)
- Ground aniseed — 5 g
- Ground cinnamon — 3 g
- Saffron, steeped in 20 ml hot water — 0.2 g
- Baking powder — 3 g
- Fine sea salt — 4 g
- Egg — 50 g (1)
- Olive oil — 50 ml
- White wine vinegar — 10 ml
- Orange blossom water — 25 ml
- Water — ~80 ml
- Honey — 500 g
- Neutral oil for frying — 1500 ml

## Method
1. Knead flour, ground sesame, aniseed, cinnamon, baking powder, salt, egg, olive oil, vinegar, saffron water, 15 ml orange blossom water and enough water for a firm, smooth dough, 8 min. Rest 30 min.
2. Roll to 2 mm; cut 8 cm squares. Make 4 parallel slits in each, leaving the edges attached.
3. Thread alternate strips over a finger and pinch the two opposite corners through and together, turning the square inside-out into a flower. Rest shaped pastries 15 min.
4. Warm honey with remaining orange blossom water to 70 °C — never boiling.
5. Fry at 165 °C, 5–6 min, to deep amber (low and slow cooks the centre).
6. Straight from the oil into the warm honey; soak 4–5 min, turning, until glossy and heavy.
7. Drain on a rack; scatter whole sesame while tacky.

## Plating & Service
Stack in a glossy pyramid; serve with mint tea (traditionally alongside harira). They keep 2 weeks in a sealed tin — the honey preserves them.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Egg-free: replace with 25 ml extra water and 5 ml oil. Dip half of each flower in dark chocolate for a franco-marocain petit four.
