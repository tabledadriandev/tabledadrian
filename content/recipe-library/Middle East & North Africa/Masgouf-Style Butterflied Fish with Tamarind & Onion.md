# Masgouf-Style Butterflied Fish with Tamarind & Onion

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Baghdad's riverside ritual translated to the grill — a whole fish butterflied flat, lacquered with tamarind, and cooked skin-side to the fire until the flesh side is amber and the edges char like bark.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- Whole sea bass or carp-style fish, butterflied from the back, spine removed — 1800 g
- Fine sea salt — 16 g
- Tamarind paste — 50 g
- Olive oil — 70 ml
- Curry-mild spice mix: ground coriander 5 g, cumin 4 g, turmeric 3 g, black pepper 2 g
- Garlic, grated — 12 g
- Onions, thick rings — 300 g
- Ripe tomatoes, halved — 400 g
- Lemon juice — 30 ml
- Flat-leaf parsley — 12 g
- Green chilli, sliced — 10 g

## Method
1. Open the butterflied fish flat like a book; score the flesh lightly and salt both sides. Rest 30 min; pat dry.
2. Whisk tamarind with oil, spices, garlic and lemon into a loose lacquer.
3. Brush the flesh side heavily; reserve a third for basting.
4. Grill skin-side to the heat (medium coals or 200 °C grill) 25–30 min, propped upright or on a rack, basting twice — traditional masgouf never touches flesh to flame until the end.
5. Flip flesh-side down for the final 3–4 min for char. Target 58 °C in the thickest part.
6. Grill onion rings and tomatoes alongside until charred and soft.

## Plating & Service
Serve the fish open and standing proud on a board, charred onion and tomato tumbled over, parsley and chilli scattered. Warm bread, amba, and pickles complete the Baghdad table.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Oven route: roast skin-down at 220 °C for 20 min, then grill 3 min. Sweeten the lacquer with 10 g date syrup for a rounder glaze.
