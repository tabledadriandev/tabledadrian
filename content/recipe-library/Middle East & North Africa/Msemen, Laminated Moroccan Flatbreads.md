# Msemen, Laminated Moroccan Flatbreads

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Square, buttery, hand-laminated — the dough is stretched to transparency on an oiled counter, folded around semolina and butter, and griddled until it shatters into layers; the souk's answer to puff pastry.

## Ingredients (serves 6, makes 12)
- Strong flour — 450 g
- Fine semolina — 50 g (dough) + 60 g (laminating)
- Fine sea salt — 9 g
- Sugar — 6 g
- Warm water — 300 ml
- Neutral oil — 150 ml (for stretching)
- Unsalted butter, very soft — 80 g
- Honey and butter, to serve — 60 g + 30 g

## Method
1. Knead flour, 50 g semolina, salt, sugar and water 10 min to a very soft, silky dough (it should barely hold shape). Rest 20 min.
2. Divide into 12 balls; coat each in oil and rest 15 min more — oil, not flour, is the medium throughout.
3. On an oiled counter, flatten and stretch one ball with oiled palms to a near-transparent 30 cm sheet, working from the centre out.
4. Dot with soft butter, dust with laminating semolina; fold sides to the middle into a square, catching air between layers.
5. Rest folded squares 10 min; press each gently to 15 cm.
6. Griddle on a plancha at 200 °C, 2–3 min per side, pressing edges, until gold with dark freckles and visibly layered.
7. Clap each one between your hands out of the pan — this separates the laminations.

## Plating & Service
Serve hot, folded in a cloth, with warm honey-butter for breakfast, or plain beside tagines to trap sauce in the layers.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Stuff before folding with 15 g spiced onion-tomato for msemen mâamrine. Dairy-free: laminate with oil only — slightly less rich, still excellent.
