# Preserved Lemons, Salt-Cured with Bay & Coriander Seed

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Four weeks of patience for years of payoff — quartered lemons packed in their own salted juice until the rind turns silky and the flavour goes deep umami-citrus; the pantry item that defines Moroccan cooking.

## Ingredients (makes 1 × 1 L jar)
- Unwaxed lemons, scrubbed — 700 g (6–7 small)
- Coarse sea salt — 140 g
- Extra lemon juice — 150–250 ml (3–4 lemons)
- Bay leaves — 2
- Coriander seeds — 4 g
- Black peppercorns — 3 g

## Method
1. Sterilise a 1 L jar (10 min at 110 °C oven or boiling water).
2. Cut each lemon almost into quarters, keeping the base attached; pack 15 g salt into the cuts of each.
3. Press lemons into the jar one by one, crushing down hard so juice rises; layer in bay, coriander and peppercorns and remaining salt as you go.
4. Top up with fresh lemon juice until completely submerged — exposed rind moulds.
5. Seal; leave at cool room temperature 3 days, inverting daily, then refrigerate or cellar 4 weeks minimum before use.
6. To use: rinse, scrape away flesh and pith, and slice or mince the rind only.

## Plating & Service
Slivered into chicken tagine, chermoula, grain salads, butter for fish; the cured juice seasons vinaigrettes and Bloody Mary rims.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Add a cinnamon stick and 2 cloves for a warmer cure. Meyer lemons or small Menton lemons cure fastest with the silkiest rind.
