# Steak Tartare, Coupé au Couteau

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Hand-cut fillet seasoned to order — knife work, ice-cold discipline and a table-side mixing bowl make this pure bistro theatre.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- Beef fillet or trimmed sirloin, very fresh — 600 g
- Egg yolks — 4 (or 40 g pasteurised yolk)
- Dijon mustard — 25 g
- Ketchup — 15 g
- Worcestershire-style sauce — 12 ml
- Tabasco — 3 ml
- Cornichons, fine dice — 60 g
- Capers, chopped — 30 g
- Shallots, fine dice — 60 g
- Flat-leaf parsley, chopped — 12 g
- Olive oil — 30 ml
- Fine salt — 6 g; black pepper 2 g
- Baguette or frites, to serve — 300 g

## Method
1. Chill the beef, boards and bowls in the freezer 20 min — everything stays under 6 °C.
2. Slice beef 5 mm, then batons, then 5 mm dice with a razor-sharp knife. Never mince or process.
3. Whisk mustard, ketchup, Worcestershire-style sauce, Tabasco and oil into a dressing.
4. Fold beef with shallots, cornichons, capers, parsley and dressing; season decisively — tartare dies underseasoned. Taste, adjust.
5. Pack into a ring on each cold plate; unring.
6. Crown with a yolk (in a half-shell for looks) or fold yolks in for a creamier bind.
7. Serve within 10 minutes of mixing.

## Plating & Service
Cold plates, frites or grilled baguette, extra Tabasco and the pepper mill on the table.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Italian-leaning: olive oil, lemon, no ketchup. Always source beef butchered same-day and keep the chain cold — this is a raw dish.
