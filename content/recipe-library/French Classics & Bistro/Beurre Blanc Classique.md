# Beurre Blanc Classique

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Nantes' butter sauce at the canonical ratio — 60 ml reduction to 150 g cold butter — the whitest test of heat control in the kitchen.

## Ingredients (serves 6, makes ~250 ml)
- Shallots, very finely minced — 50 g
- Dry white wine (Muscadet) — 100 ml
- White wine vinegar — 40 ml
- Double cream (optional stabiliser) — 15 ml
- Cold unsalted butter, 1.5 cm dice, kept chilled — 150 g
- Fine salt — 3 g
- White pepper — 0.5 g
- Lemon juice — 5 ml

## Method
1. Reduce shallots, wine and vinegar in a small heavy pan to 60 ml of moist, syrupy reduction.
2. Add the cream if using (insurance against splitting; purists skip it); bubble 20 sec.
3. Over the lowest heat, whisk in cold butter 2–3 cubes at a time, adding more only as the last melts.
4. Keep the sauce between 50 and 60 °C throughout — too hot it oils, too cold it sets.
5. Season with salt, white pepper and a thread of lemon.
6. Strain for a refined service, or keep the shallots in for bistro character.
7. Hold no more than 30 min in a warm (not hot) bain-marie, whisking occasionally.

## Plating & Service
Spoon over poached turbot, pike, scallops or steamed leeks the moment it's mounted.

## Derivatives
Beurre rouge: red wine + red vinegar. Beurre citron: all lemon. Add saffron, vanilla or yuzu for signature plates.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Split rescue: start a fresh 20 ml reduction and whisk the broken sauce back in slowly.
