# Sauce Béarnaise à l'Estragon

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Hollandaise's steak-loving sister — a tarragon-shallot reduction folded through a warm yolk emulsion; the only sauce a côte de bœuf truly needs.

## Ingredients (serves 6, makes ~350 ml)
- Egg yolks — 4 (80 g)
- Tarragon vinegar (or white wine vinegar) — 40 ml
- Dry white wine — 40 ml
- Shallots, minced — 40 g
- Tarragon — 20 g (stalks in the reduction, leaves chopped for the finish)
- Black peppercorns, crushed — 3 g
- Clarified butter, 45–50 °C — 250 g
- Chervil, chopped — 5 g
- Fine salt — 4 g
- Cayenne — 0.3 g

## Method
1. Reduce vinegar, wine, shallots, tarragon stalks and peppercorns to 25 ml; strain, pressing; cool slightly.
2. Whisk yolks with the reduction over a bain-marie to a dense sabayon at 65 °C.
3. Off the heat, stream in the clarified butter, whisking to a thick, spoon-holding emulsion.
4. Season with salt and cayenne.
5. Fold in chopped tarragon leaves and chervil.
6. Hold at 55–60 °C, up to 1 h, whisking now and then.

## Plating & Service
A generous quenelle beside grilled beef, salmon or asparagus — never refrigerate and reheat; make it à la minute.

## Derivatives
Choron: + 30 g tomato concassée. Paloise: mint for tarragon (lamb). Foyot: + 20 ml glace de viande.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Lighter: replace 50 g butter with 50 ml warm reduction of stock. Split rescue: re-emulsify into a teaspoon of warm water.
