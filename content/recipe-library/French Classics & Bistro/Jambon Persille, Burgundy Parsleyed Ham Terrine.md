# Jambon Persillé, Burgundy Parsleyed Ham Terrine

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Chunks of braised ham suspended in a sharp white-wine parsley jelly — Burgundy's Easter terrine, brilliant on a summer buffet.

## Ingredients (serves 10)
- Smoked ham hock — 1 (about 1.2 kg)
- Pork shoulder, 4 cm cubes — 400 g
- Dry white wine (Aligoté style) — 500 ml
- Water — 700 ml
- Carrot — 100 g
- Onion, clove-studded — 1 (120 g)
- Celery — 50 g
- Bay leaves — 2
- Thyme — 4 sprigs
- Gold gelatine leaves — 8 (16 g)
- Flat-leaf parsley, chopped — 60 g
- Garlic, minced — 8 g
- White wine vinegar — 25 ml
- Dijon mustard (to serve) — 30 g
- Black pepper — 2 g

## Method
1. Simmer hock and shoulder with wine, water, carrot, onion, celery, bay and thyme, lid ajar, 2.5 h until the hock collapses.
2. Strain; reduce the liquor to 600 ml, skimming. Taste — it should be well seasoned by the hock.
3. Bloom gelatine; dissolve in the hot liquor with the vinegar. Cool to 25 °C.
4. Pull the meat into rough 2–3 cm pieces, discarding skin and bone; season with pepper.
5. Stir parsley and garlic into the syrupy jelly.
6. Layer meat and jelly in a 1.2 L glass bowl or terrine, finishing with jelly. Set 6 h minimum.
7. Unmould by dipping briefly in hot water; slice thick.

## Plating & Service
Thick rustic slices with Dijon, cornichons and grilled bread; keeps 5 days chilled.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Brighter: add 15 g chopped tarragon with the parsley. No hock? Use 1.4 kg shoulder plus 10 g extra gelatine.
