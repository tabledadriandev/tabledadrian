# Pasteis de Nata with Blistered Tops and Cinnamon

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Lisbon's custard tarts — laminated dough spiralled into tins, custard poured cool, oven at maximum so the tops leopard-spot.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- Plain flour — 250 g
- Water — 130 ml
- Fine sea salt — 3 g
- Unsalted butter, softened — 175 g
- Whole milk — 300 ml
- Double cream — 100 ml
- Plain flour (custard) — 25 g
- Caster sugar — 200 g
- Water (syrup) — 100 ml
- Cinnamon stick — 3 g
- Lemon peel — 4 g
- Egg yolks — 120 g (6)
- Ground cinnamon — 2 g

## Method
1. Make a supple dough with flour, water and salt; rest 30 minutes. Roll to a rectangle, spread with a third of the butter, fold letter-style; repeat twice, chilling 20 minutes between turns.
2. Roll the dough into a tight log; chill 1 hour.
3. Boil sugar, syrup water, cinnamon stick and lemon peel to 105 °C.
4. Whisk custard flour with 60 ml cold milk; heat remaining milk and cream to 85 °C, whisk in the slurry, cook 2 minutes.
5. Stream the hot syrup into the milk base, whisking; cool 10 minutes, then whisk in yolks. Strain.
6. Cut the log into 12 discs; press each up the sides of muffin tins with wet thumbs, leaving a thin spiral base.
7. Fill three-quarters with custard; bake at 280 °C (max) for 10–13 minutes until blistered black-spotted and the custard just trembles.
8. Cool 10 minutes; dust with cinnamon.

## Plating & Service
Serve warm, always — with espresso; stack casually on a plate as in a Belém queue.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> A splash of port in the custard is heretical and delicious; commercial all-butter puff works when the sea is rough.
