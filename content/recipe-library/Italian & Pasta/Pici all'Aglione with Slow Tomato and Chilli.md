# Pici all'Aglione with Slow Tomato and Chilli

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Fat hand-rolled Tuscan noodles — flour and water only — dragged through a garlic-perfumed tomato sauce where whole cloves melt into sweetness.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- plain flour (or 00) — 400 g
- warm water — 200 ml
- olive oil for dough — 10 ml
- fine salt — 4 g
- garlic, whole peeled cloves — 8 (about 40 g)
- extra virgin olive oil — 60 ml
- San Marzano tinned tomatoes, crushed — 600 g
- dried chilli flakes — 1 g
- sugar — 3 g (if needed)
- basil leaves — 8

## Method
1. Knead flour, water, oil and salt 10 minutes; rest 30 minutes under a bowl.
2. Roll dough 1 cm thick, cut into 1 cm strips, then roll each under your palms into long 4 mm ropes. Coil on a semolina-dusted tray.
3. Halve garlic cloves and sweat them in the olive oil over the lowest heat 10 minutes — golden and soft, never fried.
4. Crush the garlic into the oil with a fork. Add chilli, then tomatoes.
5. Simmer 25 minutes until thick and jammy; balance with sugar and salt.
6. Boil pici 6–8 minutes — they should stay pleasantly chewy.
7. Toss in the sauce with a ladle of pasta water for 1 minute until coated and glossy.

## Plating & Service
Tangled high in bowls, torn basil, a thread of raw olive oil. Cheese is traditionally absent — offer pecorino only on request.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Aglione is traditionally made with the giant Val di Chiana garlic — if found, use half the quantity. Add crumbled sausage for a heartier crew meal.
