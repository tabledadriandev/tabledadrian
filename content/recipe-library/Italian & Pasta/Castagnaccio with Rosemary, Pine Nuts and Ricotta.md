# Castagnaccio with Rosemary, Pine Nuts and Ricotta

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Tuscany's austere chestnut flatcake — flour, water, oil, rosemary; barely sweet, deeply autumnal, rescued from severity by a spoon of honeyed ricotta.

## Ingredients (serves 8)
- chestnut flour, sifted — 300 g
- water — 400 ml
- extra virgin olive oil — 50 ml
- fine salt — 3 g
- caster sugar — 15 g (modern, optional)
- sultanas, soaked — 50 g
- pine nuts — 40 g
- walnuts, broken — 30 g
- rosemary needles — 2 g
- ricotta — 200 g (to serve; omit for vegan)
- honey or chestnut honey — 30 g (to serve; agave for vegan)

## Method
1. Whisk chestnut flour, salt and sugar with the water to a smooth, pourable batter — like thin crepe batter; rest 30 minutes.
2. Stir in 30 ml oil and the drained sultanas.
3. Pour 1.5 cm deep into a well-oiled 26 cm round tin.
4. Scatter pine nuts, walnuts and rosemary; anoint with remaining oil.
5. Bake at 190°C for 30–35 minutes until the surface craquelures like dry earth — the crack pattern is doctrine.
6. Cool to warm; it firms as it settles.

## Plating & Service
Warm rough wedges, a spoon of ricotta beaten with honey alongside — or naked with vin santo for the full contadino experience.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Buy new-season chestnut flour (autumn) — old flour turns bitter. The batter takes orange zest gracefully, though Tuscan grandmothers will feel it.
