# Ossobuco alla Milanese with Gremolata

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Veal shins braised until the marrow loosens, lifted at the last second by raw lemon-garlic gremolata — the mandatory partner to risotto alla Milanese.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- veal shin slices (ossobuco), 4 cm thick — 4 × 350 g
- plain flour — 40 g
- butter — 60 g
- olive oil — 30 ml
- onion, fine — 120 g
- carrot, fine — 80 g
- celery, fine — 60 g
- dry white wine — 200 ml
- veal or chicken stock — 500 ml
- tomato paste — 25 g
- lemon zest — of 2 lemons
- garlic, minced — 4 g
- parsley, fine — 12 g
- fine salt and black pepper — to taste

## Method
1. Tie each shin around its equator; snip the membrane in 3 places so they stay flat. Season, flour lightly.
2. Brown in oil and half the butter, 4 minutes per side; remove.
3. Sweat soffritto in the same pot 8 minutes; add tomato paste, 2 minutes.
4. Return meat; add wine, reduce by half; add stock to two-thirds up the shins.
5. Braise lidded at 160°C for 2–2.5 hours, turning once, until the meat trembles off the bone but the marrow still sits in the bone.
6. Lift the meat; reduce the sauce to a glossy nappe; mount with remaining butter.
7. Gremolata at the last moment: lemon zest, garlic, parsley.

## Plating & Service
One shin per warm plate over saffron risotto or soft polenta, sauce over, gremolata scattered — provide marrow spoons.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> The gremolata must be cut fresh; it dies within the hour. Pork osso buco is a fine weekday understudy at the same schedule.
