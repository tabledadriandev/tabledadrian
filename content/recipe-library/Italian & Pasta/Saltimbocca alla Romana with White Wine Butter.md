# Saltimbocca alla Romana with White Wine Butter

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> 'Jumps in the mouth' — veal, sage and prosciutto pinned together and flashed in butter; a five-minute main that tastes like Rome's centro storico.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- veal escalopes — 8 × 70 g
- prosciutto crudo, thin slices — 8
- sage leaves — 8 (plus 4 to fry)
- plain flour — 40 g
- butter — 70 g
- olive oil — 20 ml
- dry white wine — 120 ml
- chicken stock — 60 ml
- black pepper — to taste
- fine salt — barely (the ham seasons)

## Method
1. Pound escalopes between film to 4 mm.
2. Lay one sage leaf and one slice of prosciutto on each; secure with a toothpick woven flat.
3. Flour the veal side only, dusting off excess.
4. Sear in oil and 30 g butter, prosciutto-side first, 90 seconds; flip for 60 seconds; remove.
5. Deglaze with wine; add stock; reduce by half.
6. Mount the sauce with remaining cold butter; return meat 30 seconds to glaze, spooning over.

## Plating & Service
Two per plate, prosciutto up and translucent, sauce around, a crisp fried sage leaf — traditionally with sautéed spinach or roman-style peas.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Chicken or turkey breast work for lighter charters; pork loin for crew. Never salt before tasting — the ham decides.
