# Guinea Fowl al Mattone with Lemon and Sage

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Spatchcocked bird pressed under a brick over steady heat — Tuscan technique that trades basting for physics: maximum skin contact, minimum moisture loss.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- guinea fowl (or free-range chicken), spatchcocked — 1.4 kg
- extra virgin olive oil — 60 ml
- lemon — 2 (juice of 1, one halved to serve)
- garlic, crushed — 4 cloves
- sage leaves — 10
- rosemary — 2 sprigs
- black pepper, cracked — 3 g
- fine salt — 12 g
- dry white wine — 80 ml

## Method
1. Spatchcock: remove the backbone, press flat, pop the keel. Dry brine with the salt, 2 hours to overnight, uncovered in the fridge.
2. Marinate 30 minutes in oil, lemon juice, garlic, herbs and pepper; pat mostly dry.
3. Heat a heavy pan or plancha to medium. Lay the bird skin-down; weight with a foil-wrapped brick or a second heavy pan.
4. Cook 20–25 minutes skin-down, moving once, until the skin is uniformly mahogany and crisp.
5. Flip; weight again; 12–15 minutes to 72°C in the thigh.
6. Rest 10 minutes; deglaze the pan with wine and any resting juices for a spoon-over jus.

## Plating & Service
Cut into rustic quarters on a board, jus over, charred lemon halves, crisped sage from the pan.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> The brick suits the yacht grill perfectly — no oven needed. Poussins take 12 minutes per side for individual service.
