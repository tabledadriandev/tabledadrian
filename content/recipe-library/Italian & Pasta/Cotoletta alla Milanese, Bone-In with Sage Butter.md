# Cotoletta alla Milanese, Bone-In with Sage Butter

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The true Milanese — bone-in veal chop pounded wide, crumbed once, fried gold in clarified butter and basted with more; 'elephant ear' spectacle with an artisan's crust.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- veal rib chops, frenched, bone-in — 4 × 300 g
- fine dry breadcrumbs (white, no crust) — 250 g
- whole eggs — 3
- plain flour — 60 g
- clarified butter — 250 g
- whole butter — 40 g
- sage — 6 leaves
- lemon — 2, halved
- flaky salt — to taste

## Method
1. Butterfly each chop off the bone slightly and pound to 1.5 cm, keeping the bone attached — the 'orecchia d'elefante' silhouette.
2. Dry the meat. Flour lightly, dip in beaten egg, then press hard into the crumbs — press, don't dust; the crust must weld.
3. Rest crumbed chops 15 minutes, uncovered.
4. Heat clarified butter to 165°C in a wide pan, 1 cm deep.
5. Fry 3–4 minutes per side to even gold, basting the bone end; core 58°C.
6. Drop in the whole butter and sage off the heat; baste 30 seconds; drain on a rack; salt immediately.

## Plating & Service
Whole chop overhanging the plate, lemon half, nothing else — or a small rocket-tomato salad directly on top, the modern Milan way.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Never oil, never parmesan in the crumb — that's another dish. Pork chops work for crew night at identical timing.
