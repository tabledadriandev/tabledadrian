# Duck Breast with Saba, Roasted Grapes and Wet Polenta

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> North-Italian autumn on one plate — crisp-skinned duck lacquered with saba (grape must), roasted muscat grapes, and a loose parmesan polenta beneath.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- duck breasts — 4 × 200 g
- saba (or reduced balsamic) — 40 ml
- red or muscat grapes, on small stems — 300 g
- red wine — 80 ml
- chicken stock — 150 ml
- butter — 30 g
- coarse polenta — 200 g
- water and milk — 450 ml each
- Parmigiano Reggiano — 50 g
- thyme — 2 g
- fine salt and black pepper — to taste

## Method
1. Start polenta: rain into simmering salted water-milk; cook 40 minutes, stirring often; finish with Parmigiano and half the butter.
2. Score duck skin in a fine crosshatch; salt both sides.
3. Lay skin-down in a COLD pan; render over medium-low 8–10 minutes until the skin is deep amber and thin.
4. Flip 2 minutes; then roast at 180°C to core 54°C, about 4–6 minutes. Rest 8 minutes skin-up.
5. Roast the grape clusters in the duck pan with thyme, 6 minutes at 180°C.
6. Sauce: pour off fat, deglaze with wine, add stock and saba; reduce to a light lacquer; mount with remaining butter.

## Plating & Service
A pool of soft polenta, sliced duck fanned rosy-side up, grapes leaning on it, sauce brushed over the skin.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Saba is cooked grape must — sweeter and rounder than balsamic; find it in any Italian épicerie. Cherries replace grapes in June.
