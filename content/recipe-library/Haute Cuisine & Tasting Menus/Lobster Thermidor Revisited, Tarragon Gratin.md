# Lobster Thermidor Revisited, Tarragon Gratin

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The grand hotel classic, lightened: mustard-tarragon sabayon glazed over sweet lobster in its shell.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- Lobsters, live, 600 g each — 2
- Butter — 60 g
- Shallot, brunoise — 50 g
- Brandy — 25 ml
- Dry white wine — 100 ml
- Fish stock — 150 ml
- Double cream — 100 ml
- Egg yolks — 3 (54 g)
- Dijon mustard — 15 g
- Tarragon, chopped — 8 g
- Gruyere, grated — 40 g
- Cayenne — 0.3 g
- Fine salt — 5 g

## Method
1. Blanch lobsters 4 minutes, ice, halve lengthwise, clean the heads and crack the claws; extract claw meat and dice the tail meat large, keeping shells pristine.
2. Sweat shallot in butter; flambe with brandy, add wine and reduce by half.
3. Add stock and 60 ml cream; reduce to 120 ml of sauce, season with mustard, tarragon, cayenne and salt.
4. Fold the lobster meat through the sauce off the heat and refill the half-shells.
5. Whip yolks with 20 ml water over a 70°C bain-marie to ribbons; fold in remaining 40 ml cream, softly whipped.
6. Spoon the sabayon over each shell and scatter Gruyere.
7. Glaze under a hot salamander 2–3 minutes until burnished; the meat inside must not exceed 58°C.

## Plating & Service
Serve the half-shell on hot salt with lemon and nothing else; the gratin should be leopard-spotted, not brown.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Langoustines or king prawn split-shells scale it down for canapés; skip the cheese for a lighter glaze.
