# Venison Loin, Sauce Grand Veneur & Poached Quince

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Blood-rare venison with the peppered royal-hunt sauce and honeyed quince — autumn's most serious plate.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- Venison loin, trimmed — 700 g
- Red wine — 300 ml
- Game or beef jus — 200 ml
- Redcurrant jelly — 30 g
- Double cream — 40 ml
- Black peppercorns, crushed — 4 g
- Juniper, crushed — 4 berries
- Shallot, sliced — 60 g
- Red wine vinegar — 20 ml
- Quince — 2 (500 g)
- Honey — 40 g
- Star anise — 1
- Butter — 60 g
- Fine salt — 6 g

## Method
1. Poach peeled quince quarters in 500 ml water with honey and star anise at 90°C for 25–35 minutes until tender and rose-gold.
2. Reduce wine, vinegar, shallot, juniper and pepper to 60 ml; add jus, reduce to coating.
3. Strain; whisk in redcurrant jelly, then cream — grand veneur should be glossy, peppery, faintly sweet.
4. Temper and season the loin; sear hard in 30 g butter on all faces, 4 minutes total.
5. Roast at 180°C to a core of 50°C, about 6–8 minutes; rest 10 minutes — it should serve at rare-plus.
6. Caramelise quince faces in remaining butter.
7. Carve thick medallions, season the faces, and plate with quince and the sauce.

## Plating & Service
Dark plates, few elements, sauce poured at the table; venison waits for no reheating — timing is the dish.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Loin of lamb takes the same sauce; pear stands in for quince at a fraction of the poaching time.
