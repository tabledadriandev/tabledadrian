# Slow-Cooked Salmon, Sorrel Beurre Blanc

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Salmon held at 46°C until it eats like silk, with the sharp green lift of sorrel — a quiet modern classic.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- Salmon fillet, centre-cut, skinless — 4 x 160 g
- Olive oil — 30 ml
- White wine — 80 ml
- White wine vinegar — 20 ml
- Shallot, brunoise — 40 g
- Cold butter, diced — 140 g
- Double cream — 20 ml
- Sorrel, shredded — 40 g
- Baby spinach — 60 g
- Fleur de sel — 4 g
- White pepper — 0.5 g

## Method
1. Brine salmon 10 minutes in 5% salt water; dry and oil.
2. Cook at 46°C in a water bath (vacuum-sealed) or a 90°C oven with a probe, to a core of 44–46°C — about 20–25 minutes.
3. Reduce wine, vinegar and shallot to 25 ml; add cream.
4. Mount with cold butter over low heat; season with salt and white pepper.
5. Fold shredded sorrel into the sauce at the last moment — it will soften and turn khaki within minutes, so move fast.
6. Wilt spinach in a hot pan, season.
7. Plate spinach, salmon (season the top with fleur de sel), and generous ribbons of sorrel beurre blanc.

## Plating & Service
The salmon should look almost raw and taste utterly cooked; serve on warm, not hot, plates to protect the texture.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Sea trout is even finer; spinach plus lemon replaces sorrel where unavailable — add it the same way.
