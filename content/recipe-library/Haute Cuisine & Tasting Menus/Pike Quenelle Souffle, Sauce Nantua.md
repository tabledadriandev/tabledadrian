# Pike Quenelle Souffle, Sauce Nantua

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Cloud-light pike dumplings that souffle in the oven under a crayfish cream — Lyon's grandest dish, done properly.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- Pike fillet, boneless — 300 g
- Whole milk — 125 ml
- Butter — 100 g
- Plain flour — 75 g
- Whole eggs — 150 g (3)
- Double cream — 150 ml
- Crayfish or prawn shells — 300 g
- Brandy — 20 ml
- Tomato paste — 25 g
- Fish stock — 500 ml
- Nutmeg — 0.3 g
- Cayenne — 0.3 g
- Fine salt — 8 g

## Method
1. Make a panade: boil milk with 50 g butter, beat in flour, dry 2 minutes, cool completely.
2. Blend pike with 5 g salt; add panade, then eggs one by one; pass through a tamis.
3. Beat in 100 ml cold cream over ice; season with nutmeg. Chill 2 hours — the mix must be pipeable but firm.
4. Sauce: roast shells in 20 g butter, flambe with brandy, add tomato paste and stock, simmer 30 minutes, strain, reduce to 200 ml, finish with 50 ml cream and 30 g butter; season with cayenne.
5. Shape quenelles between two large spoons and poach in 80°C salted water for 10 minutes, turning once.
6. Drain, lay in a buttered gratin dish, coat with sauce Nantua.
7. Bake at 200°C for 15–18 minutes until swollen by half and blistered gold — serve before they sigh.

## Plating & Service
From oven to table in ninety seconds; the quenelles collapse slowly, so guests must be seated and ready.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Whiting or zander replace pike; a fine steamed rice pilaf is the only correct accompaniment.
