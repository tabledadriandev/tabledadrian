# Charred Leek, Hazelnut Sabayon & Cured Egg Yolk

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> A humble leek burnt to sweetness, gilded with warm nut sabayon — vegetable-forward fine dining at its best.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- Baby leeks — 12 (500 g)
- Egg yolks (to cure) — 4
- Fine salt (cure) — 200 g
- Caster sugar (cure) — 100 g
- Egg yolks (sabayon) — 3 (54 g)
- Brown butter, warm — 90 g
- Hazelnut oil — 15 ml
- White wine — 40 ml
- Lemon juice — 10 ml
- Toasted hazelnuts, crushed — 30 g
- Chive oil — 15 ml

## Method
1. Five days ahead: bury 4 yolks in the salt-sugar cure; refrigerate 4 days, rinse, then dry at 60°C for 8 hours until grateable.
2. Char whole leeks in a dry cast-iron pan or plancha at full heat, 12–15 minutes, until the outer layers are black.
3. Finish in a 180°C oven for 10 minutes until the hearts are juicy-tender; rest wrapped 5 minutes.
4. Strip off the burnt jackets to reveal the pale, smoky hearts; keep warm and season.
5. Whisk 3 yolks with wine over a bain-marie at 70°C to thick ribbons, 5 minutes.
6. Stream in brown butter and hazelnut oil; season with lemon and salt.
7. Nap the leeks with sabayon, scatter hazelnuts, grate cured yolk generously over, finish with chive oil.

## Plating & Service
Serve on warm dark plates, leeks in a strict parallel row, sabayon glazed under a hot grill for 30 seconds if you want colour.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Skip the cured yolk and use smoked almonds for a faster build; the leek jackets can be dried and powdered as a garnish.
