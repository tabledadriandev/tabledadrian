# Bigos — Polish Hunter's Stew of Two Cabbages

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The stew that improves for three days: sauerkraut and fresh cabbage collapsed with three meats, smoked sausage, prunes and juniper — Poland's national slow burn.

## Ingredients (serves 8)
- Sauerkraut — 800 g
- White cabbage, shredded — 600 g
- Pork shoulder, diced — 500 g
- Beef chuck, diced — 300 g
- Smoked kiełbasa, thick-sliced — 300 g
- Smoked bacon, diced — 150 g
- Onions, diced — 250 g
- Dried porcini — 20 g
- Prunes, halved — 100 g
- Dry red wine — 200 ml
- Tomato paste — 40 g
- Juniper berries, crushed — 6
- Bay leaves — 3
- Allspice berries — 4
- Marjoram — 4 g
- Salt — 10 g
- Black pepper — 3 g

## Method
1. Soak porcini in 300 ml warm water. Render bacon in a 7 L pot; brown pork and beef in the fat in batches; set meats aside.
2. Soften onion in the pot, 8 minutes; add tomato paste for 2.
3. Return meats with kraut (rinsed once, squeezed), fresh cabbage, porcini with liquor, wine, juniper, bay, allspice and 400 ml water.
4. Simmer, covered, at the lowest blip 2.5 hours, stirring occasionally from the bottom.
5. Add kiełbasa, prunes and marjoram; simmer uncovered 30 minutes until thick, brown-bronze and jammy at the edges. Season.
6. Cool overnight if you can — bigos law says day three is the summit; reheat gently.

## Plating & Service
Deep bowls with rye bread and a cold beer or frozen vodka; garnish is unnecessary and arguably illegal.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Venison or duck legs make hunter's bigos literal; keep total meat ~40% of cabbage weight for balance.
