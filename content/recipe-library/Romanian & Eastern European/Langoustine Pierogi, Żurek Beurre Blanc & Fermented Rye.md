# Langoustine Pierogi, Żurek Beurre Blanc & Fermented Rye

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Poland's two icons in one plate: silk pierogi filled with langoustine mousse, sauced with a beurre blanc soured by fermented rye zakwas — coastal and ancestral at once.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- Langoustine tails, shelled — 300 g
- Whipping cream, cold — 100 ml
- Egg white — 20 g
- Lemon zest — 2 g
- Plain flour — 300 g
- Warm water — 150 ml
- Sunflower oil — 15 ml
- Rye zakwas (fermented rye starter, see żurek) — 120 ml
- Shallots, minced — 50 g
- Dry white wine — 100 ml
- Cold butter, diced — 150 g
- Marjoram — 1 g
- Salt — 10 g
- White pepper — 1 g
- Trout roe — 40 g
- Dill — 6 g

## Method
1. Mousse: blitz 200 g cold langoustine with 3 g salt and egg white; pulse in cream; fold in remaining tails diced, zest, white pepper. Chill.
2. Dough: knead flour, water, oil, 4 g salt, 8 minutes; rest 30 minutes. Roll to 1.5 mm.
3. Cut 8 cm rounds; fill with 18 g mousse, seal into half-moons with a tight rope edge. Hold on semolina.
4. Beurre blanc: reduce wine and shallot to 30 ml; add 80 ml zakwas, reduce by a third; whisk in cold butter at 60–70°C; season, add a pinch of marjoram, hold at 50°C.
5. Poach pierogi in barely simmering salted water 3 minutes after floating — the mousse needs gentleness, not a boil.
6. Drain onto warm plates.

## Plating & Service
Four pierogi per plate, beurre blanc spooned over, trout roe and dill fronds — the sauce should taste unmistakably of żurek's sour soul.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Scallop mousse substitutes 1:1; if zakwas is beyond reach, buttermilk plus 5 ml cider vinegar approximates the lactic sourness.
