# Marhapörkölt with Nokedli — Hungarian Beef Paprika Stew

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The stew Hungarians actually mean by 'goulash abroad': beef and onions collapsed into a thick paprika gravy, no water it didn't earn, over slippery nokedli.

## Ingredients (serves 6)
- Beef shin or chuck, diced 3 cm — 1 kg
- Onions, finely diced — 400 g
- Lard or sunflower oil — 60 g
- Sweet Hungarian paprika — 30 g
- Hot paprika — 3 g
- Caraway, ground — 3 g
- Garlic — 20 g
- Tomato — 150 g
- Hungarian wax pepper — 150 g
- Dry white wine — 100 ml
- Salt — 14 g
- Plain flour (nokedli) — 300 g
- Eggs (nokedli) — 2 (110 g)
- Sour cream, optional — 100 g

## Method
1. Sweat the onions in lard over low heat 20 minutes to deep gold — pörkölt lives or dies on the onion base (40% of the meat's weight, minimum).
2. Off heat, stir in both paprikas; immediately add beef, salt and 50 ml wine.
3. Cover; braise at the lowest simmer, mostly in its own juices, 2–2.5 hours, adding splashes of water only when it threatens to catch.
4. At the 1-hour mark add chopped tomato, sliced wax pepper, crushed garlic, caraway and remaining wine.
5. Uncover for the final 20 minutes to reduce to a clinging, brick-red gravy.
6. Nokedli: beat flour, eggs, 4 g salt and 120 ml water to a thick batter; press through a spätzle sieve into boiling salted water, lift when they float, toss with a little lard.

## Plating & Service
A ladle of pörkölt over nokedli, optional sour cream, wax pepper rings; the gravy should coat, never flood.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Pork shoulder cuts the time to 90 minutes; venison pörkölt with juniper is the hunting-lodge variant.
