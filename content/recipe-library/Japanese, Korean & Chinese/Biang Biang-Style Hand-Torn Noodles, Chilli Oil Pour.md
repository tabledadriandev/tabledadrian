# Biang Biang-Style Hand-Torn Noodles, Chilli Oil Pour

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Wide, hand-ripped ribbons under a hiss of hot oil over chilli, garlic and spring onion — primal noodle theatre.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- Strong white flour — 400 g
- Water — 210 ml
- Fine salt — 6 g
- Neutral oil (dough) — 15 ml
- Gochugaru or coarse Sichuan chilli flakes — 20 g
- Garlic, minced — 20 g
- Spring onion, fine — 40 g
- Sichuan pepper, ground — 2 g
- Light soy — 40 ml
- Black vinegar — 30 ml
- Caster sugar — 8 g
- Neutral oil (pour) — 90 ml
- Pak choi, halved — 200 g
- Toasted sesame seeds — 8 g

## Method
1. Knead flour, water and salt 8 minutes to a stiff, smooth dough; rest 30 minutes.
2. Divide into 8 pieces; roll into fat batons, oil generously, and rest under film 60 minutes minimum — this is the secret.
3. Whisk soy, vinegar and sugar; divide between wide bowls.
4. Flatten each baton, press a chopstick groove down the centre, then holding both ends, slap and stretch to a metre-long ribbon; tear along the groove.
5. Boil ribbons 60–90 seconds with the pak choi; drain into the bowls.
6. Top each with chilli flakes, garlic, spring onion and Sichuan pepper; pour 20 ml smoking (200°C) oil over each to sizzle. Toss and serve.

## Plating & Service
The oil pour happens in front of guests, one bowl at a time — the crackle is the dish's name spoken aloud.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Cumin lamb (see mains) on top turns it into a feast; the dough also cuts into thick knife-cut noodles if slapping feels risky at sea.
