# Twice-Cooked Pork Belly, Leeks & Black Bean

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Hui guo rou — belly simmered, chilled, sliced thin and returned to a violent wok until the edges frill and catch.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- Pork belly, skin on — 600 g
- Ginger — 15 g
- Spring onion — 20 g
- Shaoxing wine — 30 ml
- Doubanjiang — 30 g
- Fermented black beans, rinsed — 12 g
- Sweet bean sauce (tianmianjiang) or hoisin — 15 g
- Soy sauce — 10 ml
- Caster sugar — 8 g
- Neutral oil — 25 ml
- Leeks or baby leeks, bias-cut — 250 g
- Red pepper, diamonds — 80 g
- Garlic, sliced — 10 g

## Method
1. Simmer the belly whole with ginger, spring onion and wine 35 minutes to 75°C core; chill thoroughly (ideally overnight).
2. Slice 2–3 mm thin — chilling makes this possible.
3. Sear the slices in oil in a smoking wok until the fat renders and edges curl and brown, 3 minutes; pour off excess fat.
4. Push aside; fry doubanjiang, black beans and garlic 45 seconds in the fat.
5. Add sweet bean sauce, soy and sugar; toss to lacquer the pork.
6. Add leeks and pepper; stir-fry 90 seconds — leeks should stay half-crisp.

## Plating & Service
Rustic, glossy, straight to a warm platter; steamed rice and something plain and green alongside.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Use pointed cabbage instead of leek in the Chengdu manner; the pre-simmered belly holds 3 days, making this a 6-minute à la minute dish.
