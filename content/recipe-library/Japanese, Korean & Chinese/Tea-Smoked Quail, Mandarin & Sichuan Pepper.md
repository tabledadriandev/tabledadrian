# Tea-Smoked Quail, Mandarin & Sichuan Pepper

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Lapsang and mandarin peel smoke over jasmine rice — glazed quail with crisp skin and a perfumed, rosy breast.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- Quails — 4
- Fine salt — 8 g
- Caster sugar — 8 g
- Five-spice — 2 g
- Jasmine rice (for smoking) — 60 g
- Lapsang souchong tea — 20 g
- Dried mandarin or fresh peel — 10 g
- Brown sugar — 30 g
- Honey — 25 g
- Soy sauce — 15 ml
- Sichuan pepper, ground — 1.5 g
- Mandarin segments — 2 fruits
- Watercress — 40 g

## Method
1. Dry-brine quails with salt, sugar and five-spice 2 hours, uncovered, in the fridge.
2. Line a wok with foil; add rice, tea, peel and brown sugar. Set a rack above.
3. Heat until the mix smokes steadily; add quails, lid tightly, and smoke 12 minutes at medium heat.
4. Whisk honey, soy and Sichuan pepper for the glaze.
5. Roast smoked quails at 200°C for 8–10 minutes, glazing twice, to 58°C in the breast; rest 5 minutes.
6. Halve and serve with mandarin segments and watercress dressed in the resting juices.

## Plating & Service
Half a quail per starter, skin lacquered and up, mandarin bright against the smoke.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Poussin works with +12 minutes roasting; use oolong and orange for a softer smoke profile.
