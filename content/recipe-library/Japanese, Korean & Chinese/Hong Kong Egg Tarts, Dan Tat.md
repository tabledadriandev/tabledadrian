# Hong Kong Egg Tarts, Dan Tat

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Glassy, trembling egg custard in crumbly short pastry — the tea-house classic, baked small and served just-warm.

## Ingredients (serves 8)
- Plain flour — 220 g
- Icing sugar — 45 g
- Cold butter — 130 g
- Egg yolk — 1
- Cold water — 15 ml
- Fine salt — 2 g
- Eggs (custard) — 3
- Caster sugar — 90 g
- Hot water — 180 ml
- Evaporated milk — 90 ml
- Vanilla — 2 ml

## Method
1. Rub flour, icing sugar, salt and butter to fine crumbs; bind with yolk and water. Rest 1 hour.
2. Roll 4 mm; line 8 fluted tart tins (8 cm), pressing well up the sides; chill 30 minutes.
3. Dissolve sugar in hot water; cool. Whisk in eggs, evaporated milk and vanilla — gently, no foam.
4. Strain twice; rest 10 minutes and skim.
5. Fill shells to 90%; bake at 200°C for 10 minutes, then 160°C for 12–15 minutes until the custard domes softly and a toothpick stands in the centre.
6. If it puffs, crack the oven door. Cool 15 minutes in the tin.

## Plating & Service
Serve just-warm with jasmine tea — the custard should shiver, the pastry should shatter.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Puff-pastry (bought, all-butter) shells for the flaky Macau-adjacent style; brûlée the top and it becomes Macanese in spirit.
