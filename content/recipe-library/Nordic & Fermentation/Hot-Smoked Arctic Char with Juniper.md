# Hot-Smoked Arctic Char with Juniper

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Blush-pink char smoked warm over juniper and alder — served just-flaking with brown butter, it's Lapland on a plate.

## Ingredients (serves 8)
- Arctic char fillets, skin on, pin-boned — 900 g
- Filtered water — 1000 ml
- Fine sea salt — 80 g
- Brown sugar — 40 g
- Juniper berries, crushed — 5 g
- Bay leaf — 1 g
- Alder chips + a few dried juniper sprigs — 60 g
- Lemon — 60 g

## Method
1. SAFETY: brine is 8% salt; brine time 45–60 minutes only (fillets, not whole fish); hot-smoke to an internal temperature of 63°C minimum; eat within 3 days refrigerated at 2–4°C.
2. Dissolve salt and sugar in the water with juniper and bay; chill the brine fully.
3. Brine fillets 45–60 minutes at 2–4°C; rinse, pat dry.
4. Dry on a rack, uncovered, in the fridge 2 hours to form a pellicle.
5. Hot-smoke at 80–90°C over alder and juniper, 35–45 minutes, to 63°C internal at the thickest point.
6. Rest 5 minutes; the flakes should just separate under a fingertip.
7. Serve warm, or chill for salads and smørrebrød.

## Plating & Service
Serve a warm fillet with foaming brown butter, lemon and dill-dressed new potatoes; flake leftovers into a skagen-style toast.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Trout is the direct substitute; glaze with birch syrup in the final 10 minutes for a lacquered festive version.
