# Lacto-Fermented Blueberries with Juniper

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Sour-sweet, fizzy berries that lift game, duck, skyr desserts and cheese boards — the juice alone is a finished sauce.

## Ingredients (serves 12)
- Blueberries, ripe and unblemished — 1000 g
- Fine sea salt (non-iodised) — 20 g
- Juniper berries, lightly crushed — 4 g
- Lemon zest strips (no pith) — 2 g
- Vacuum bag or 1.5 L sterilised jar — 1
- For service: cold-pressed rapeseed oil — 20 ml
- Cracked black pepper — 1 g
- Small mint leaves — 5 g

## Method
1. SAFETY: use exactly 2% salt by weight of fruit (20 g per 1000 g); ferment at 21–24°C away from sunlight; total fermentation time 4–5 days.
2. Rinse berries, drain well, toss gently with salt, juniper and lemon zest until salt dissolves on the fruit surface.
3. Vacuum-seal at 99% in a bag with room to expand, or pack into the jar and press a zip bag of brine on top so all fruit stays submerged.
4. Ferment at 21–24°C. From day 2, taste daily: the bag will balloon; burp and reseal (or skim the jar) each day.
5. Stop at day 4–5 when the berries are lightly fizzy, sour but still fruity, and juice is pink-purple. pH should read 4.0 or below; if it smells of alcohol or cheese, discard.
6. Refrigerate at 2–4°C to arrest fermentation; the flavour holds for 3 weeks. Strain and reserve the juice separately.
7. To serve as a condiment, dress 200 g berries with rapeseed oil, pepper and mint.

## Plating & Service
Spoon over seared duck, venison or a skyr parfait; brush the strained juice onto grilled bread with goat cheese.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Swap juniper for lemon thyme or crushed pink pepper; blend the finished berries with their juice for a probiotic coulis.
