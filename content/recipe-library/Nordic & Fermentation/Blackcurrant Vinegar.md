# Blackcurrant Vinegar

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Inky, tannic and perfumed — the Nordic answer to red-wine vinegar, made for venison, beets and dark chocolate desserts.

## Ingredients (serves 25)
- Blackcurrants, fresh or frozen, crushed — 800 g
- Filtered water — 1000 ml
- Caster sugar — 150 g
- Champagne yeast — 1 g
- Live vinegar mother or unpasteurised cider vinegar — 100 ml
- Sterilised 3 L jar, muslin, band — 1

## Method
1. SAFETY: aerobic, cloth-covered, 20–26°C; total fermentation time 7–9 weeks; keep fruit cap punched down twice daily in stage one to prevent surface mould.
2. Dissolve sugar in water; add crushed currants and yeast.
3. Cloth cover; ferment 12–14 days, punching the fruit cap down morning and night. It should smell like young cassis wine.
4. Strain and press the fruit firmly; add the mother to the liquid.
5. Cloth cover; rest 5–7 weeks at 20–26°C, stirring weekly, tasting from week 4.
6. Stop at deep, sour cassis with a dry tannic finish.
7. Bottle; optional 70°C/10 min pasteurisation; keeps over a year in the dark.

## Plating & Service
Reduce to a gastrique for venison loin; dress roasted beets with it warm; one teaspoon electrifies a chocolate sorbet.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Blackberries give a softer version; the pressed pulp cooks into a sharp fruit leather for cheese boards.
