# Cold-Smoked Salmon

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Silk-textured, oak-whispered salmon that never saw heat — the technique that separates a galley from a smokehouse.

## Ingredients (serves 16)
- Salmon side, skin on, pin-boned, pre-frozen — 1400 g
- Coarse sea salt — 140 g
- Caster sugar — 70 g
- Brown sugar — 30 g
- Crushed black pepper — 4 g
- Oak or beech dust for cold smoke generator — 100 g
- Rapeseed oil (finishing) — 10 ml

## Method
1. SAFETY: fish must be frozen at -20°C for 24 h beforehand (parasites); cure is 10% salt (140 g per 1400 g) — do not reduce, it is your pathogen hurdle; smoke temperature must stay BELOW 27°C at all times (smoke at night or over ice); total process 3 days: 24 h cure + 12 h pellicle + 8–12 h smoke + 12 h rest; keeps 7 days at 2–4°C or freeze.
2. Mix salt, sugars and pepper; coat the flesh evenly, thicker over the loin.
3. Cure at 2–4°C for 24 hours (no weight — keeps the texture silkier).
4. Rinse quickly, pat dry, and dry on a rack, uncovered, at 2–4°C for 12 hours until tacky (the pellicle — smoke won't take without it).
5. Cold-smoke with oak dust 8–12 hours, keeping chamber under 27°C; add ice trays in warm weather.
6. Rest wrapped at 2–4°C for 12–24 hours — the smoke mellows and distributes.
7. Wipe with a film of oil; carve long, translucent horizontal slices.

## Plating & Service
Serve barely dressed: lemon, cracked pepper, rye; or drape over warm blinis with crème fraîche at the champagne hour.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> 4 hours of smoke gives a delicate 'breakfast' style; juniper wood in the dust reads instantly Nordic.
