# Yellow Pea Miso

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Split yellow peas — the soul of Nordic soup culture — fermented into a honeyed, deeply savoury miso that defines the house style.

## Ingredients (serves 40)
- Dried yellow split peas — 1000 g
- Fresh barley koji — 1000 g
- Fine sea salt (non-iodised) — 240 g
- Reserved pea cooking liquor — 200 ml
- Extra salt for surface dusting — 20 g
- Sterilised 3 L crock or food-safe bucket — 1
- Cling film and 2 kg weight — 1

## Method
1. SAFETY: salt is 12% of the pea+koji weight (2000 g = 240 g) — never go below 10% for a long room-temperature ferment; age at 18–24°C; total fermentation time 90–120 days minimum; a white surface bloom (kahm) is scraped off, but green/black mould means trimming 2 cm deep or discarding.
2. Soak peas 8 hours; simmer 60–90 minutes until they crush easily but aren't mushy. Drain, reserving liquor. Cool to 30°C.
3. Grind or mash peas coarsely. Mix thoroughly with crumbled koji and salt, adding liquor until the paste just holds a fingerprint (aim 50–52% hydration feel).
4. Knead into fist-sized balls and throw them hard into the crock to eliminate air pockets; level the surface.
5. Dust the top with extra salt, press cling film onto the surface, add the weight.
6. Age at 18–24°C. Check monthly: pour off and reserve any dark tamari that rises; re-cover.
7. From day 90, taste — sweet, salty, deeply savoury with stone-fruit notes. Age to 6–12 months for darker character.
8. Refrigerate at 2–4°C once you like it; keeps over a year.

## Plating & Service
Whisk into butter sauces, glaze salmon or roots, or loosen with dashi for a Nordic miso soup.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Rice koji makes it gluten-free; the skimmed tamari is a finished seasoning — bottle it separately like treasure.
