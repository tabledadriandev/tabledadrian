# Kassler-Style Cured and Smoked Pork Loin

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Brine-cured, beech-smoked pork loin — rosy, sliceable, equally at home hot with braised cabbage or cold on rye.

## Ingredients (serves 8)
- Pork loin, trimmed, in one piece — 1500 g
- Filtered water — 1500 ml
- Fine sea salt — 105 g
- Curing salt #1 (nitrite, optional but traditional for colour) — 4 g
- Brown sugar — 50 g
- Juniper berries — 4 g
- Bay leaves — 2 g
- Black peppercorns — 3 g
- Beech chips — 80 g

## Method
1. SAFETY: 7% salt brine, refrigerated at 2–4°C, for 72 hours; if using curing salt #1, keep it at 0.25% of meat weight maximum (4 g here) — weigh precisely; hot-smoke/roast to 65°C core; rest to 68°C; keeps 5 days chilled.
2. Simmer brine ingredients (except chips) 3 minutes; chill completely below 4°C.
3. Submerge the loin fully; brine 72 hours at 2–4°C, turning daily.
4. Rinse, pat dry; fridge-dry uncovered 12 hours.
5. Hot-smoke at 110°C over beech for 60–90 minutes to 65°C core.
6. Rest 15 minutes under loose foil.
7. Slice thick for hot service or chill overnight and shave thin for smørrebrød.

## Plating & Service
Hot: with apple-braised red cabbage and fermented mustard. Cold: on rye with lacto cucumber and crisp onions.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Omit nitrite for a grey-but-delicious 'fresh' version eaten within 3 days; a maple glaze in the last 15 minutes suits festive menus.
