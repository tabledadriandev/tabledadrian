# Black Garlic

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Six patient weeks turn garlic into balsamic-liquorice jewels — the pastry-dark umami that bridges savoury and dessert.

## Ingredients (serves 30)
- Whole garlic heads, firm, papery, unblemished — 12 heads (approx. 600 g)
- Beeswax wrap or foil — as needed
- Rice cooker or dehydrator holding 60°C — 1
- For black garlic purée: aged cloves — 100 g
- Water — 40 ml
- Sherry vinegar — 10 ml
- Fine sea salt — 2 g

## Method
1. SAFETY: this is enzymatic aging (Maillard at low temperature), not microbial fermentation — hold at a constant 60°C with 70–80% humidity; total transformation time 40–45 days; below 55°C spoilage becomes possible, above 70°C they dry and scorch.
2. Leave heads whole and unpeeled; wrap loosely in beeswax wrap or a double foil layer to retain moisture.
3. Nest in the rice cooker on 'keep warm' (verify 60°C with a probe) or a dehydrator at 60°C.
4. Leave 40–45 days. Check weekly: the kitchen note is molasses and tamarind by week 3.
5. Done when cloves are jet-black, soft as dates, sweet-sour with no raw bite.
6. Air-dry unwrapped heads 1 day; store airtight at room temperature 3 months or refrigerated 6.
7. For purée: blend cloves with water, vinegar and salt until glossy; pass; pipe from a squeeze bottle.

## Plating & Service
Dot purée beside lamb or beetroot; slice whole cloves onto flatbreads with skyr; fold into brownies for a haunting dessert.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Black shallots work the same in 30 days; blend purée with butter for an instant steak finisher.
