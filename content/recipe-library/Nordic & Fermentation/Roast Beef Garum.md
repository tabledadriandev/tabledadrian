# Roast Beef Garum

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Trim and offcuts become a jet-black amino sauce that seasons like soy but tastes of aged beef — a teaspoon transforms any jus.

## Ingredients (serves 40)
- Beef trim and mince (lean, fresh) — 1000 g
- Fresh barley koji — 250 g
- Filtered water — 800 ml
- Fine sea salt (non-iodised) — 240 g
- Sterilised 2 L jar with loose lid — 1
- Muslin and fine chinois for straining — 1

## Method
1. SAFETY: salt is 12% of TOTAL weight (2050 g ≈ 240 g) — never below 10% for meat ferments; hold at 60°C throughout (a warm-water circulator or rice cooker), which suppresses pathogens while enzymes work; total fermentation time 8–10 weeks; discard on any putrid (not savoury-funky) smell.
2. Brown the mince hard in a dry pan to deep mahogany, about 15–20 minutes; cool slightly. Roasting first gives a 'roast dinner' register.
3. Combine beef, crumbled koji, water and salt in the jar; stir well.
4. Hold at 60°C, lid loose, stirring twice weekly with a clean spoon; the liquid darkens and clears over the weeks.
5. From week 8, taste: it should hit like beef soy sauce — salty, roasted, endless finish.
6. Strain through muslin-lined chinois without pressing; let it drip 2 hours.
7. Optional polish: warm to 70°C for 10 minutes, skim, and pass again for a crystal sauce.
8. Bottle; refrigerate at 2–4°C. Keeps 6 months.

## Plating & Service
Season tartare, glaze roasted onions, or add 5 ml to any pan sauce at the finish — treat it as liquid seasoning, not a pour-over.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Skip browning for a cleaner, lighter garum; rice koji keeps it strictly gluten-free (as tagged) — verify your koji.
