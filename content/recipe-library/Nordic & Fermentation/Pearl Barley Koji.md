# Pearl Barley Koji

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The mother culture of the whole Nordic-ferment programme — sweet, chestnut-scented barley that powers misos, garums and shio koji.

## Ingredients (serves 20)
- Pearl barley — 1000 g
- Koji-kin spores (Aspergillus oryzae, barley strain) — 2 g
- Rice flour (to disperse spores) — 10 g
- Filtered water for soaking — 2000 ml
- Clean linen cloth — 1
- Perforated hotel pan or wooden koji tray — 1

## Method
1. SAFETY: koji grows at 27–30°C and must NEVER exceed 42°C internal or the mould dies; total culture time 44–48 hours; use scrupulously clean equipment; discard if you see black, green or pink patches or smell ammonia.
2. Soak barley in water 4 hours; drain 1 hour until surface-dry.
3. Steam (not boil) at 100°C for 40 minutes until grains are cooked through but discrete and springy.
4. Spread on a tray; cool to 30°C. Mix spores with rice flour and dust evenly through the grain with gloved hands.
5. Mound the grain, cover with damp linen, hold in a chamber at 28–30°C with 70–75% humidity (an oven with the light on and a water tray works).
6. At 18–24 hours the grain smells sweetly of mushroom; break up the mound, level to 3 cm, furrow it, re-cover. Check temperature every 6–8 hours — turn if above 38°C.
7. At 44–48 hours the barley is bound in white mycelium and smells of chestnut and grapefruit. Done.
8. Use fresh within 4 days (refrigerated at 2–4°C), or dehydrate at 45°C / freeze for storage.

## Plating & Service
Not a dish itself: shave dried koji over sauces like parmesan, or deploy it in shio koji, misos and garums.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Use short-grain rice with rice-strain spores for gluten-free koji; toasted koji blended into warm cream makes an instant dessert base.
