# Celery Vinegar

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Savoury, green and saline — this is the vinegar for oyster mignonette and white-fish crudo when lemon is too obvious.

## Ingredients (serves 25)
- Celery juice, fresh-pressed and strained — 1000 ml
- Caster sugar — 80 g
- Champagne yeast — 1 g
- Live vinegar mother or unpasteurised cider vinegar — 100 ml
- Celery leaves (added at bottling) — 5 g
- Sterilised 2 L jar, muslin, band — 1

## Method
1. SAFETY: aerobic ferment, cloth cover only; hold at 20–26°C; total fermentation time 5–7 weeks; juice must be fresh and chilled until you start; discard if it ever smells rotten rather than sour.
2. Warm 100 ml of the juice to 30°C, dissolve the sugar, and whisk back into the rest with the yeast.
3. Cloth-cover the jar; ferment 7–10 days at 20–26°C until it tastes like a dry celery 'wine' and bubbling subsides.
4. Add the vinegar mother; re-cover with muslin.
5. Rest at 20–26°C, stirring weekly, 4–6 weeks; taste from week 3.
6. Stop when sharply sour but still tasting of fresh celery stalk.
7. Bottle over a few celery leaves; refrigerate at 2–4°C to keep the green top-notes (they fade at room temperature).

## Plating & Service
Mignonette: 50 ml celery vinegar, 20 g shallot, 1 g pepper over oysters; or whisk with oil for shaved-fennel salads.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Cucumber juice ferments identically for a cooler profile (no celery allergen); a 50/50 blend of both is elite with crab.
