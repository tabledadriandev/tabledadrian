# Lacto Plum and Bay Ferment

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The workhorse of the larder: fizzy, sweet-sour plum wedges plus a purple juice that glazes duck, pork and roasted roots.

## Ingredients (serves 14)
- Firm ripe plums (quetsche or mirabelle), stoned, quartered — 1200 g
- Fine sea salt (non-iodised) — 24 g
- Fresh bay leaves, bruised — 2 g
- Black peppercorns, whole — 2 g
- For plum glaze: strained ferment juice — 150 ml
- Honey or agave (for glaze) — 30 g
- Cold butter or vegan butter (for glaze) — 40 g
- Sherry vinegar (for glaze, to adjust) — 5 ml

## Method
1. SAFETY: 2% salt on fruit weight; ferment at 21–24°C; total fermentation time 4–6 days; discard if mould, sliminess or solvent smell appears.
2. Toss plum quarters with salt, bay and peppercorns until juices start to draw.
3. Vacuum-seal flat in a single layer at 99%, or pack in a sterilised jar pressed under a brine bag.
4. Ferment at 21–24°C. Burp the bag daily from day 2 as CO2 builds.
5. Taste at day 4: target is prickly, plummy and sour like a good lambic; pH 4.0 or below. Stop by day 6 at the latest.
6. Refrigerate at 2–4°C. Strain juice off; keep wedges and juice separately up to 4 weeks.
7. For the glaze: reduce 150 ml juice with honey to 80 ml over medium heat, whisk in cold butter off the heat, adjust with vinegar.

## Plating & Service
Fan raw fermented wedges over pâté or duck breast; nap the glaze on roast pork belly at the pass.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Apricots ferment identically (4 days); for keto service skip honey and mount the reduced juice with butter only.
