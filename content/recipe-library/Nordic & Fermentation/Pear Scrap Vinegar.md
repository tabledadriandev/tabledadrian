# Pear Scrap Vinegar

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Peels and cores from the pastry section become a perfumed vinegar that outclasses anything you can buy for dressings.

## Ingredients (serves 30)
- Ripe pear scraps (peels, cores) or chopped whole pears — 800 g
- Filtered water — 1200 ml
- Caster sugar — 120 g
- Unpasteurised cider vinegar with mother (starter) — 100 ml
- Sterilised 3 L wide-mouth jar — 1
- Muslin cloth and elastic band — 1

## Method
1. SAFETY: this is a two-stage AEROBIC ferment — cover with cloth, never a sealed lid; ferment at 20–26°C; total fermentation time 6–8 weeks (2 weeks alcoholic + 4–6 weeks acetic); finished vinegar must taste sharply acidic (target ~5% acidity) before room-temp storage.
2. Dissolve sugar in the water; add pear scraps to the jar and cover with the sweet water.
3. Cover with muslin. Stir twice daily for 10–14 days as wild yeast makes a light pear wine (it will bubble and smell boozy). Keep fruit pushed under.
4. Strain out solids. Stir in the live vinegar starter.
5. Re-cover with muslin; leave at 20–26°C, stirring weekly. A gel-like mother may form — leave it.
6. Taste from week 4 of this stage: done when clean, bright and sharply sour with pear perfume (6–8 weeks total).
7. Bottle; it stabilises at cellar temperature. Pasteurise at 70°C for 10 minutes if you want to stop all activity.

## Plating & Service
Whisk 1:3 with rapeseed oil for salads; deglaze pork pans; a splash over strawberries with black pepper is a two-minute dessert.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Apple scraps work identically; keep the mother to launch the next batch — it accelerates everything.
