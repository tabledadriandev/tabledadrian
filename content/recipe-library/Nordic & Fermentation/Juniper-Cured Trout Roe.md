# Juniper-Cured Trout Roe

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Popping amber pearls with a whisper of gin-forest — house 'caviar' for blinis, crudo and buttered rye.

## Ingredients (serves 12)
- Fresh trout roe skeins, impeccably fresh — 400 g
- Filtered water (for the separating bath, 40°C) — 1000 ml
- Fine sea salt (bath) — 30 g
- Filtered water (curing brine) — 500 ml
- Fine sea salt (curing brine) — 50 g
- Juniper berries, crushed — 3 g
- Caster sugar — 10 g

## Method
1. SAFETY: work with same-day roe kept below 4°C; the curing brine is a 10% salt solution for 15–20 minutes; finished roe keeps only 4–5 days at 0–2°C — label the tin.
2. Make the warm bath: 40°C water with 30 g salt. Submerge skeins; gently rub the membrane against a rack or your fingertips so eggs fall free.
3. Skim and discard membrane; drain eggs in a fine sieve.
4. Simmer curing brine ingredients 2 minutes; chill completely to below 4°C; strain out juniper.
5. Submerge eggs in the cold brine 15–20 minutes — taste at 15; they should be saline, popping, faintly resinous.
6. Drain 30 minutes in a sieve, chilled.
7. Pack into a sterile jar or tin; hold at 0–2°C.

## Plating & Service
A generous spoon over warm blinis with crème fraîche; or crown a slice of cold-smoked salmon and watch the table go quiet.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Salmon roe cures identically at 20 minutes; skip juniper and add lemon zest for a cleaner French profile.
