# Rye and Barley Shoyu

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> A Nordic soy sauce brewed on rye and barley — brighter and maltier than commercial shoyu, and entirely ours.

## Ingredients (serves 50)
- Dried soybeans — 500 g
- Rye grain, cracked — 250 g
- Pearl barley, cracked — 250 g
- Koji-kin spores (soy/shoyu strain) — 2 g
- Filtered water (for the moromi brine) — 1500 ml
- Fine sea salt (non-iodised) — 345 g
- Sterilised 4 L crock — 1
- Muslin for pressing — 1

## Method
1. SAFETY: the moromi brine is 23% salt on the water (1500 ml = 345 g) giving ~15% in the total mash — this high salt is what keeps a year-long room-temperature ferment safe; koji stage must stay below 42°C; total fermentation time 6–12 months.
2. Soak soybeans 12 hours; steam 60 minutes until crushable. Toast rye and barley in a dry pan until nutty; crack coarsely.
3. Cool everything to 30°C, mix, inoculate with spores, and grow koji at 28–30°C for 44–48 hours (turn at 24 hours; never above 42°C).
4. Dissolve salt in the water; add the finished koji mash. This is the moromi.
5. Ferment in the crock at 18–25°C, stirring once a day for the first month, then weekly.
6. The mash darkens and smells of caramel and dried fruit; taste from month 6.
7. When rounded and deeply savoury (6–12 months), pour into muslin and press hard overnight.
8. Optionally pasteurise the liquid at 80°C for 10 minutes for a stable, glossy sauce; bottle and refrigerate at 2–4°C, 1 year.

## Plating & Service
Use anywhere soy sauce belongs; it makes a rye-echoing glaze for salmon brushed on in the last minute of cooking.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> The pressed cake seasons crackers and rye dough; there is no realistic gluten-free version of this one — use tamari-style pea shoyu instead.
