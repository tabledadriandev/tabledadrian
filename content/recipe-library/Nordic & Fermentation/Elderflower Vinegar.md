# Elderflower Vinegar

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> June in a bottle — heady floral acidity that turns a butter sauce or a bowl of strawberries into an event.

## Ingredients (serves 25)
- Fresh elderflower heads, shaken clean (not washed) — 60 g
- Filtered water — 1000 ml
- Caster sugar — 130 g
- Lemon, sliced — 50 g
- Live vinegar mother or unpasteurised cider vinegar — 100 ml
- Sterilised 2 L jar, muslin, band — 1

## Method
1. SAFETY: aerobic, cloth-covered ferment at 20–26°C; total fermentation time 6–8 weeks; use only fully open, creamy-smelling flower heads; strip all thick green stems (mildly toxic raw).
2. Dissolve sugar in water; add flowers and lemon; the wild yeast on the blossoms starts the ferment.
3. Cloth cover; stir twice daily for 10–14 days until it smells like dry floral wine.
4. Strain out flowers and lemon; stir in the mother.
5. Cloth cover; rest at 20–26°C, 4–6 weeks, tasting weekly from week 3.
6. Stop at bright, perfumed sourness; bottle.
7. Store cool and dark; refrigerate after opening. Keeps 1 year.

## Plating & Service
Finish beurre blanc for turbot with 10 ml; splash over strawberries and cream; brighten chilled pea soup at the pass.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Dried elderflower (20 g) works off-season; rose petals (unsprayed) make a sister vinegar for lamb dressings.
