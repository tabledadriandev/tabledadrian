# Cured and Cold-Smoked Duck Ham

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Duck breast cured, smoked and air-dried into rosy 'ham' — carved thin it rivals any charcuterie board ashore.

## Ingredients (serves 10)
- Duck breasts, large, skin on — 2 (approx. 700 g)
- Coarse sea salt — 70 g
- Caster sugar — 35 g
- Juniper berries, crushed — 4 g
- Black pepper, cracked — 4 g
- Orange zest — 3 g
- Bay leaf, crumbled — 1 g
- Beech dust for cold smoking — 60 g
- Muslin for hanging — 1

## Method
1. SAFETY: cure is 10% salt for 24–36 hours at 2–4°C; cold-smoke below 27°C; then air-dry at 12–15°C and 70–75% humidity (a wine fridge works) for 12–18 days until 30% weight loss — weigh at the start and write it on the muslin; discard if any wet, sticky patches or off smells develop.
2. Mix salt, sugar, juniper, pepper, zest and bay.
3. Bury the breasts in the cure, flesh side down; cure 24 h (36 h if over 400 g each) at 2–4°C.
4. Rinse, pat dry; air-dry in the fridge 12 hours to form a pellicle.
5. Cold-smoke 6 hours below 27°C over beech.
6. Wrap each breast in muslin; hang or rack at 12–15°C, 70–75% RH.
7. Weigh from day 10; done at 30% loss (12–18 days): firm but with a little give at the centre.
8. Rest wrapped in the fridge 2 days; slice paper-thin at 45° across the breast.

## Plating & Service
Shingle slices with lacto plum wedges and toasted rye; or drape over a warm lentil salad so the fat turns translucent.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Skip smoking for a pure 'duck prosciutto'; a fennel-seed cure sends it Riviera-ward.
