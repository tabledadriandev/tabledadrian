# Isomalt Herb Glass Tuiles

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Stained-glass windows with herbs frozen mid-fall — isomalt stays clear and resists sea humidity far better than sucrose.

## Ingredients (serves 16)
- Isomalt — 200 g
- Water — 20 ml
- Small soft herbs (sage, chervil, micro basil, fennel frond) — 24 leaves
- Edible flowers, petals — 12
- Fine salt — 0.5 g (for savoury glass)
- Silicone mats — 2

## Method
1. Melt isomalt with the water to 165–170 °C — pale and water-clear; do not colour it.
2. Rest 1 minute off heat to let bubbles rise.
3. Spoon 10 g pools onto a silicone mat; tilt to thin ovals.
4. Press one dried-surface herb leaf into each pool; drizzle a thread of isomalt over to laminate.
5. Optional: re-warm the sheet at 150 °C for 2 minutes and press flat with a second mat for windowpane thinness.
6. Cool completely; snap edges clean.
7. Store between parchment with silica, airtight — they hold 5 days even aboard.

## Plating & Service
Plant upright in purées, lean over gnudi, or roof an amuse. Backlight them on the pass — guests photograph the glass, not the protein.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Savoury: dust with tomato powder before the laminating thread. Sucrose version (200 g sugar, 40 g glucose, 20 ml water to 160 °C) tastes sweeter but dies faster in humidity.
