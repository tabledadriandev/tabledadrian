# Nasturtium Oil and Chlorophyll Extraction

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The greenest oil on the boat — peppery nasturtium blitzed hot-and-fast, then the same session's chlorophyll paste for tinting pasta and emulsions.

## Ingredients (serves 20)
- Nasturtium leaves (or 70/30 spinach-nasturtium) — 150 g
- Grapeseed oil — 250 ml
- Fine salt — 1 g
- Ice — for shocking
- Water (chlorophyll run) — 300 ml

## Method
1. Blanch leaves 8 seconds; shock in iced water; squeeze absolutely dry (water = cloudy oil).
2. Blitz leaves with oil in a high-power blender 2.5 minutes — friction takes it to ~60 °C, exactly the extraction sweet spot.
3. Hang through muslin or coffee filter overnight in the fridge; do not press.
4. Bottle the clear green oil; keeps 2 weeks chilled, 2 months frozen.
5. Chlorophyll: blend the spent pulp with the 300 ml water; strain; heat the liquid to 75 °C — green curds rise.
6. Lift the curds onto muslin; this paste is pure plant pigment.
7. Salt the paste lightly; freeze in 5 g nubs.

## Plating & Service
Oil: dots on soups, crudo, burrata. Chlorophyll: knead a nub into pasta dough or whisk into a mayonnaise for unnatural-looking, fully natural green.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Same protocol: basil, dill, wild garlic, carrot top, fig leaf (skip blanch for fig). Label and date everything — green oils fade quietly.
