# Master Basic Spherification Caviar — Any Juice

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The house template for pearls from any low-calcium juice — pin this ratio card to the inside of the dry-store door.

## Ingredients (serves 20)
- Clear juice or infusion (low calcium, pH above 3.6) — 500 ml
- Sodium alginate — 5 g (1.0% of the flavour base)
- Caster sugar or salt — to season, 1–2%
- Calcium chloride — 5 g (0.5%)
- Still low-calcium water (bath) — 1000 ml
- Clean water (rinse) — 500 ml
- Citric acid buffer: sodium citrate — 1–2 g (only if juice is acidic, pH < 3.6)
- Optional colour: beta-carotene drops — 2

## Method
1. Blend juice with alginate (1.0%) at high speed 60 seconds; if the juice is acidic, first stir in sodium citrate (0.2–0.4%) to raise pH above 3.6 or the alginate will not hydrate.
2. Rest the base 2 h refrigerated (or vacuum 5 minutes) until bubble-free — bubbles make comet-tailed pearls.
3. Dissolve calcium chloride in the bath water (0.5%); hold at 18–22 °C.
4. Load a syringe or caviar-maker; drip from 3–4 cm height.
5. Bathe 60–90 seconds — thin skin, liquid heart. Longer = gummy.
6. Lift with a slotted spoon; rinse 20 seconds in clean water.
7. Hold up to 4 h submerged in their own juice; skins keep thickening slowly, so serve same-day.

## Plating & Service
Deploy on oysters, soups, cocktails, desserts. Standard garnish load: 8–12 pearls per portion.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Dairy/alcohol/high-calcium liquids: switch to reverse spherification (2.0% calcium lactate gluconate in the base, 0.5% alginate bath). Log every new juice's pH in the notebook.
