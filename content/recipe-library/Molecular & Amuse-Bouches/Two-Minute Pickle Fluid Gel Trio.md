# Two-Minute Pickle Fluid Gel Trio

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Three acid gels — cornichon, pickled ginger, mustard-dill — batched from jar brines you already carry; instant acidity dots for fatty plates.

## Ingredients (serves 16)
- Cornichon brine, strained — 150 ml
- Pickled ginger brine (gari) — 150 ml
- Dill pickle brine — 130 ml
- Dijon mustard — 15 g
- Fresh dill — 5 g
- Caster sugar — 15 g (5 g per gel, adjust to each brine's bite)
- Agar-agar — 3.9 g total (0.9% per gel: 1.35 g / 1.35 g / 1.2 g)
- Water — as needed to soften over-sharp brines (up to 20%)

## Method
1. Taste each brine; dilute any that sting past pleasant with up to 20% water; add sugar to balance.
2. Gel one: boil cornichon brine with agar (0.9%) 60 seconds; set.
3. Gel two: same with ginger brine.
4. Gel three: same with dill brine; blend in mustard and dill after setting.
5. Blend each set slab separately to a glossy gel; sieve; bottle and label.
6. Acid note: these brines are near pH 3 — boil the agar fully (90 °C+) and work fast; if a gel sets weak, re-boil with 0.1% more agar.
7. All three keep 7 days chilled.

## Plating & Service
Cornichon gel with pâté and rillettes; ginger gel on crudo and tuna; mustard-dill beside cured salmon — acid that stays exactly where you pipe it.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Kimchi-brine gel (0.9%) for fried chicken canapés. This is the zero-waste answer to every empty pickle jar aboard.
