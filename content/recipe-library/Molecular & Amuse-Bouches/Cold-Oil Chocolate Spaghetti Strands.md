# Cold-Oil Chocolate Spaghetti Strands

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> Agar chocolate "spaghetti" set in a cold-oil column and coiled like pasta — a dessert garnish that makes the table lean in.

## Ingredients (serves 10)
- Water — 250 ml
- Cocoa powder — 30 g
- Caster sugar — 60 g
- Dark chocolate 70%, chopped — 40 g
- Fine salt — 0.5 g
- Agar-agar — 3.2 g (1.0% of the liquid — strands must survive handling)
- Grapeseed oil, chilled to 2–4 °C in a tall container — 750 ml
- Cocoa nibs — 10 g

## Method
1. Chill the oil column in the freezer until viscous-cold (2–4 °C).
2. Whisk water, cocoa, sugar, salt; boil with agar (1.0%) 90 seconds; melt in the chocolate off heat; strain.
3. Hold the base at 50 °C (working window: 45–55 °C).
4. Load a squeeze bottle with a 2 mm tip (or use a syringe + tubing for true spaghetti).
5. Pipe long streams into the cold oil — strands set as they sink.
6. Lift gently after 1 minute; rinse in cold water; drain on cloth.
7. Coil nests of 5–6 strands; chill up to 24 h; scatter nibs at service.

## Plating & Service
Twirl a nest beside chocolate crémeux or on a coffee dessert; serve with a fork for the pasta joke to land.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Fruit spaghetti: any juice at 1.0% agar, same column. Tubing method: chill filled tube 3 min, then blow strands out with the syringe.
