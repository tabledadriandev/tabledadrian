# Sous-Vide Quail Egg, Pancetta Powder Cloud

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> A 63 °C quail egg — barely-set white, molten yolk — under a snowfall of maltodextrin pancetta powder that melts back to bacon on the tongue.

## Ingredients (serves 12)
- Quail eggs — 12
- Pancetta — 100 g
- Tapioca maltodextrin — 45 g (about 60% of the rendered fat weight)
- Fine salt — 1 g
- Parmesan, grated — 30 g
- Chives — 4 g
- Brioche crumbs, toasted — 40 g
- Butter — 10 g
- Black pepper — 0.5 g

## Method
1. Cook quail eggs sous-vide at 63.0 °C for 45 minutes; hold in 50 °C water for service.
2. Render pancetta slowly over low heat 10–12 minutes; strain — reserve ~70 g liquid fat and the crisp lardons.
3. Whisk maltodextrin (approx. 60% of fat weight) into the warm fat with salt until a dry powder forms; adjust with more malto if greasy; sieve.
4. Toast brioche crumbs in butter until golden; season.
5. Crack each egg carefully into a warm porcelain spoon (the white just holds).
6. Scatter crumbs and a few lardons around the egg; a little parmesan.
7. Snow the pancetta powder over the top; finish with chives and pepper.

## Plating & Service
Serve warm and immediately — instruct guests to take the whole spoon; the powder vanishes into bacon fat as it meets the yolk.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Vegetarian: brown-butter powder instead (same ~60% malto-to-fat ratio) with smoked salt. GF: omit brioche, use buckwheat crunch.
