# Xanthan Nappe — The No-Cook Sauce Thickener

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> The percentage ladder for xanthan — from a whisper of body to spoon-coating nappe — without heat, starch, or flavour loss.

## Ingredients (serves 16)
- Any liquid: juice, vinaigrette, jus, coulis — 500 ml
- Xanthan gum — see ladder:
- 0.05–0.1% (0.25–0.5 g): anti-crystal insurance in sorbets, suspension in vinaigrettes
- 0.2% (1 g): light nappe — coats a spoon, pours clean
- 0.3–0.4% (1.5–2 g): thick coulis, plate-stable dots
- 0.5%+ (2.5 g): gel-like; usually too snotty — stop before here

## Method
1. Weigh the liquid; calculate the dose on a 0.01 g scale — xanthan errors are exponential.
2. Shear it in with a stick blender 30–45 seconds; never whisk by hand (fish-eyes).
3. Rest 15–20 minutes; shear entrains air that clouds sauces.
4. Vacuum or settle to de-bubble for glossy presentation.
5. Taste — xanthan is neutral but dulls acid slightly at 0.3%+; re-season.
6. It is shear-thinning: the sauce flows when poured, stands when still — plate accordingly.
7. Stable hot or cold, frozen or thawed; adjust nothing.

## Plating & Service
Use for cold jus on carpaccio, suspending herbs in dressings, and giving body to consommés that must cling to a spoonful.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Pair 0.1% xanthan with 0.4% agar in fluid gels for elasticity. It's the yacht's most-used powder — decant into a labelled shaker.
