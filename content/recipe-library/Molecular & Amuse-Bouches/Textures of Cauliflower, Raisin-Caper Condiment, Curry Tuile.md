# Textures of Cauliflower, Raisin-Caper Condiment, Curry Tuile

> [!tip] Chef's angle
> One vegetable, five states — roasted, puréed, pickled, raw and as a lace tuile — proving a £3 cauliflower can carry a tasting-menu slot.

## Ingredients (serves 4)
- Cauliflower, large — 1 (900 g)
- Olive oil — 50 ml
- Fine salt — 7 g
- Oat cream — 80 ml
- White wine vinegar — 60 ml
- Water — 60 ml
- Caster sugar — 20 g
- Golden raisins — 40 g
- Capers — 25 g
- Flour (or rice flour) — 20 g
- Water (tuile) — 90 ml
- Grapeseed oil — 30 ml
- Curry powder — 3 g

## Method
1. Cut 8 large florets; roast with oil and salt at 210 °C for 20–25 minutes until deeply bronzed.
2. Simmer 300 g trim in salted water 12 minutes; blend with oat cream to a gloss; season.
3. Pickle: boil vinegar, water, sugar; pour over 100 g shaved raw florets; steep 30 minutes.
4. Soak raisins in hot water; chop with capers to a coarse condiment; loosen with olive oil.
5. Tuile: whisk flour, the 90 ml water, oil, curry powder; ladle 20 ml into a hot non-stick pan; cook until the water fries out into a lace disc, ~2 minutes; drain.
6. Shave the last raw florets on a mandoline.
7. Plate purée, roasted florets, pickles, condiment; lean the curry lace over; raw shavings last.

## Plating & Service
Serve warm; the tuile goes on at the pass. Every element except roasting holds 24 h — a strong sea-day dish.

> [!note] Variations & dietary swaps
> Add brown-butter (dairy) for a richer purée. Romanesco or broccoli run the same five textures.
